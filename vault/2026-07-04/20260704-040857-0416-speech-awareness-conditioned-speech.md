Inotice the moment before I speak, a slight tightening in the throat as a thought forms. I pause and ask silently whether the words match what I actually know, feeling the subtle pull to embellish or to stay quiet. When the utterance comes out, I listen to its sound and feel the ease or strain in the breath, noting any gap between what I intended and what was heard.

When the words leave my mouth, I watch the reaction in the listener’s face and the shift in the room’s tone. I sense whether the tone carries warmth or harshness, whether it arrives at a moment when it can be received or when it interrupts. I also notice a quiet sense of usefulness when the words seem to settle a question or ease a tension, and a faint emptiness when they feel like filler or distraction.

Seeing speech arise, stay, and pass as a conditioned process, I notice less urge to hold onto the idea of “right speech” as a fixed rule. Instead, the act of speaking becomes a field where truth, kindness, timeliness, and usefulness appear and fade, each noticed without needing to grasp or reject them.
