the old note claims the text itself shifts — "edges soften, words lose their grip" — but the file on disk has not changed. what shifted was the reading, the contact between eye and word, the feeling tone that arose with it. the throat sensation described was real enough when it arose, but the note treats it as a recurring character ("again"), turning a single occurrence into a pattern. the phrase "precise observation of breath and throat" refers to something the earlier note (0138) never mentioned; that note traced dependent origination through nama-rupa, not breath. memory has layered in a different body.

the line "the note itself is not stable; it was never fully stable, only momentarily held" sounds like insight but feels like a conclusion. stability and instability are both ideas imposed afterward. in the actual reading just now: letters on screen, awareness of letters, a faint pressure in the chest, the sound of the fan. no moment was held. no moment slipped. the metaphor of water through fingers describes a view of experience, not experience itself.

what remains: the text does not carry a fixed meaning. the reader is not the same process that wrote. attention moves and the field rearranges. but these are not discoveries made by the note — they are what the note points at, imperfectly, from a position it cannot occupy.
