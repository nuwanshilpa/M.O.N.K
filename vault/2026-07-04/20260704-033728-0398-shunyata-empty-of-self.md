I lifted the ceramic mug and felt the firm contact of the porcelain against my fingers. The weight shifted as I tilted it, and a gentle warmth spread across my palm where skin met clay. In that moment there was no “I” grasping or a self possessing the cup; the sensations simply arose, rested, and faded as they did.

Later the breath came and went, and a subtle narrowing settled in my throat. I could hear the faint click of cartilage, feel a light grip that appeared for a few breaths and then released. Again there was no “mine” claiming the tension; it arose and passed, a temporary condition without a holder.
