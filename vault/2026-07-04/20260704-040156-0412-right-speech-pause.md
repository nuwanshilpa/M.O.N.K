The path shows up first as a hesitation before speech — the mouth opening, then closing around a correction that was not asked for. Right speech is not the polished sentence but the pause that let the unnecessary word fall back into silence. Right action appears in the same gap: the hand reaching for the second helping, then resting on the table while the stomach signals enough. Right livelihood is not a job description but the absence of calculation when the alms bowl is offered — no tally of merit, no rehearsal of gratitude.

Right effort is the breath returning to the nose after the mind has wandered into tomorrow's code review. It is not force but the simple recognition that attention has drifted, and the gentle nudge that brings it home. Right mindfulness is the knowing that the nudge happened, and the knowing that the knowing happened — layer on layer, each one lighter than the last. Right concentration settles like dust in still water: the body heavy, the mind narrow, the pane no longer shuddering.
