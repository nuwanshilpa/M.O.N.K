The grip on the thought of the unfinished task was not the task itself but the effort to keep it from slipping away. In the moment the mind reached for the next item, a thin line of tension tightened across the chest, as if the body were trying to hold the thought in place. When the thought faded, the tension loosened without any deliberate release, showing how the grip forms and falls on its own.

The loosening did not come from deciding to let go; it arose simply because the thought ceased to be present. That fleeting shift made clear how one condition gives rise to the next, and how the ending of one opens space for the next to appear or not. No effort was needed beyond noticing the change.
