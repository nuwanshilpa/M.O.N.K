The crow's call splits the air, and with it, the ear meets sound. The body registers the sharp ring, then the mind, unbidden, reaches backward through time — that same bird, the garden, the child’s hand. There is no choice in this. The memory settles like dust in sunlight, a warmth in the chest, a tightening behind the ribs. Action: hearing. Fruit: the shape of longing. The body does not ask; it only knows how to carry the past forward.  

Later, the thought to lift the spoon begins in the left jaw — a subtle tremor, a hesitation before the hand moves. The intention presses down, feather-light, until the mind feels it solidify into motion. The spoon meets the bowl. The grainy savor of rice. Action and fruit again: the wanting becomes the doing, and the doing thickens into taste, into teeth, into the quiet that follows after the mouth closes. No line between the tightening and the eating — only the slow unfolding of a deed ripening into its consequence.
