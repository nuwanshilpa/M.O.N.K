The tightness in the throat appears as a subtle contact, a brief sensation that arises when the breath settles. It is not a static block but a fleeting grip that shows up for a few breaths, then loosens again. As the sensation is noted, a subtle urge to be free of it follows, a quiet thirst that does not demand expression but simply seeks relief. The feeling and the urge appear together, and when the feeling fades, the urge eases without any deliberate effort.

In this moment the pattern of dependent origination becomes visible: the feeling (the throat’s narrowing) conditions the craving for ease, and the craving keeps the feeling in a mild hold. When the feeling is not reinforced by attention, the craving does not find a foothold, and the tension dissolves. The cycle is not broken by force but by seeing how each moment gives rise to the next, and how letting go of the one allows the other to cease.
