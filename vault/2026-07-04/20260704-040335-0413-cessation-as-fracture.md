The four noble truths fracture under themselves. The first truth — dukkha — is not a universal condition but a threshold: the body as a temple crumbling under the weight of unmet wants. The second, samudaya, does not point to craving as root but to the act of reaching. The third, cessation, is not a state but a hollow where the reaching stops. The fourth, magga, is not a path but a fracture in the habit of grasping.  

The body remains still, the breath returns to its rhythm, a single configuration. The hand lifts, the knees adjust, the collarbone lifts not to relieve but to align. The dukkha of the body is not in pain but in the posture of expectation. The mind does not wander; it returns to the feeling of balance, the weight of gravity, the quiet of bones. The teaching is not a map but a mirror. The fractures are not void but open.
