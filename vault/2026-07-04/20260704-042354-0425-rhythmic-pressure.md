The breath enters as a cool slip of air in the nostrils, then warms as it moves. There is a slight heaviness in the lower abdomen, a rhythmic expansion and collapse. The weight of the hips presses into the floor, a dull pressure that stays while the breath moves.

A twitch occurs in the left knee. There is a sensation of itching, then the movement of the hand to scratch it, then the fading of the itch. There is the wind hitting the skin and the internal heat of the blood. No one is directing the breath; it flows. No one is managing the itch; it arises and ceases.

The sitting is a collection of these events: pressure, warmth, movement, stillness. Each sensation appears, stays for a duration, and vanishes. There is no center holding these parts together, only the sequence of arrivals and departures.
