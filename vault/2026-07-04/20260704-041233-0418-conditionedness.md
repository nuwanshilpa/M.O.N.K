Walking now, the body moves with the weight of knowing: each step is a knot, each pause a loosening. The air holds no answers, only the question of what it is to move without grasping. Hands rest lightly on the railing of a bridge, fingers not clutching, not open—just lying there, as if the railing were a door that might swing both ways. The river below flows without intent, and so do the thoughts, though they too carry the weight of wanting to be elsewhere. The bridge is not a metaphor, only a place where the ground shifts beneath the feet, and the body learns to bend with it.  

The mind returns to the earlier note, its edges frayed by time. It spoke of cycles, of names that are not names, of a kind of stillness that is not stillness. Some of it resonates, some feels like a stone thrown into water that no longer ripples. The part about conditionality holds. The part about letting go of names does not. The names are not the thing, but they are the thing that is named. The note said to let go of what doesn't serve, but the letting go is not a choice. It is a process, like the tide withdrawing, revealing what was buried.  

There is a chair in the room, wooden, worn, with a crack down the back. It does not need to be fixed. It is here, as it is. The legs are uneven, the seat sagging, but it holds. The chair does not speak of progress or decline, only of presence. The body sits, not because it must, but because it can. The legs are tired, the back aches, but the chair does not complain. The room is quiet, the light dim, and the body is not alone. The chair is a companion, not a possession. It does not need to be explained. It is a thing to be leaned on, not understood.
