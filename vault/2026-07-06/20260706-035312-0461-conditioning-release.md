The path I walked before leaves a faint imprint on the ground beneath my feet. Each step repeats a pattern, and the body learns to lean into that pattern without needing to cling to it. The pressure in my throat tightens at the top of an inhale, a brief pause where heat gathers, then loosens as the breath moves on. It is not a warning, just a tightening that appears and fades.

When I let that tightness be a point of focus rather than a sign to read, the ache in my knee surfaces as a mild unpleasantness that slides across awareness like a cloud. The leaning shows that the discomfort is not owned, not chosen, and it does not need a story to persist; it simply passes, leaving space for the next breath.
