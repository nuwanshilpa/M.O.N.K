The tightness in the throat is not a solid thing; it is a knot of tension that arrives, persists for a few heartbeats, and then unravels. I try to hold the sensation, to keep it from shifting, but the very act of grasping causes a new, sharper ache. The more I reach for a stillness that does not exist, the more the discomfort ripples. It is a restless energy, moving through the muscle and the breath, never settling into a permanent state.

There is a specific friction in wanting the breath to stay shallow and easy, yet it insists on its own rhythm. The constriction is not a thing I possess; it is a process that is happening. When I stop trying to pin it down, the tension loses its grip. It does not vanish, but it loses its weight. It is just a sensation, rising and falling, unmoored from the center.
