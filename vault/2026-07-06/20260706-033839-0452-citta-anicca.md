In this sitting, the mind appears as a current without a shore. When I try to hold it still—tightening focus on the breath’s pull or the weight of the back—it resists. The breath comes in soft waves, like water sloshing in a bowl, and with each rise and fall, the mind shifts: tighter, looser, craving a control it cannot grasp. There is no self behind the eyes, only the act of looking, which itself becomes a texture—itchy, warm, sudden. This is the mark of impermanence: even the intention to observe fades into another layer
