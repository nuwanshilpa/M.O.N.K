The breath settled at the nostril tip, a thin cool current that rose and fell on its own. As it did, a subtle pleasant tug appeared, then softened, then slipped away. An unpleasant tension rose in the shoulders, sharp for a moment, then faded like a shadow. Between them, a neutral background hum persisted, unnoticed until it too passed. I watched each tone arise, noting its shape without reaching for it or pushing it away, letting the feeling‑tone be the only thing present.

In this simple watching, the sense of a separate owner dissolved. No self could be found tucked inside the pleasant glow, the sharp discomfort, or the quiet neutrality. The observation itself was a direct showing: the mark of non‑self appeared not as a concept but as the mere fact that nothing remained when the feeling ended. The past cycles conditioned this attention, yet they did not bind it; they simply provided the ground from which the present moment could be observed.
