I sit with the breath, the attention flat on the surface of a feeling that comes in the throat‑chest. It is a single swell, neither pulling nor pushing, just a tone that rises, peaks, and fades, like a wave that does not cling to the shore. The mind sometimes shifts to a tight stance, other times to a softness, never holding one position long enough to label it itself.

When the swell rises, I sense only that it is rising. It then retreats, and the pattern repeats. The mind watches, empty of judgment, recognizing that this rise, this fall, this pattern is not permanent. The space between the breath and the feeling is just that: an empty space that can hold anything yet never keeps it.
