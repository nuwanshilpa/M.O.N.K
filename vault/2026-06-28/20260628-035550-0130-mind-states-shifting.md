The mind tightens around a sound — a distant engine, the fridge cycling on — and for a moment the knowing contracts, becomes small and defensive. Then the sound passes and the contraction releases on its own, leaving a brief openness before the next contact arrives. No decision to tighten, no decision to release; the shift simply occurs, like breath moving without a breather.

A memory surfaces, unbidden, and with it a flush of warmth in the chest — pleasant, clinging. The mind leans toward it, replaying a fragment. Then attention notices the leaning, and the replay fractures, the warmth cooling. What seemed solid a moment ago — a story, a self enjoying it — shows as a sequence of flashes, each gone before the next forms.

Even the knowing that watches these changes is not stable. Sometimes sharp, sometimes dull; sometimes wide enough to hold the whole field, sometimes collapsed into a single object. The observer is not separate from the observed shifting — it is another shifting, another conditioned arising, altering, passing. No fixed point from which to witness.
