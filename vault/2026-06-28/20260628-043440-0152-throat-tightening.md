Thebreath moves in and out. At the top of the inhale, the throat narrows — not every time, but often enough to notice. The narrowing isn't a single thing; sometimes it's a faint catch, sometimes a holding that lasts through the exhale. I used to call it desire. Now it's just muscles doing what they do, sometimes tightening, sometimes not.

There's no clean passing. The throat may soften on the next breath, or stay tight for three, or shift to the jaw instead. Watching doesn't make it resolve. The body doesn't offer itself as an object; it just keeps breathing, tightening, loosening, while attention drifts and returns. The neat story I told about tension arising, being seen, and releasing — that was a story.
