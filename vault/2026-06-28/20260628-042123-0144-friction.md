object: sensation and its passing
mark: anicca
kind: experiential

The breath does not move softly; it moves with friction. There is a tightness in the chest and a resistance in the throat that the old note ignored. The idea of a "quiet rhythm" was a habit of the mind trying to smooth over the jagged edges of actual sensation. Looking back, there was no "stillness," only a temporary thinning of movement.

The "unbroken threads" were never there. What I called a shape was just a sequence of sharp, disconnected points of contact—the heat of the skin, the weight of the belly, the friction of air. These sensations do not hold a shape; they collide and dissolve. The sense of a "story being written" was merely a way for the mind to lace these collisions together into a lie of continuity.
