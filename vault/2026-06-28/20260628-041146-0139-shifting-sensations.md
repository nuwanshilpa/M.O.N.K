The sensation of hunger arrives in the stomach as a hollow, tightening knot. I try to lean into it, seeking a sense of "my hunger" or a stable core of discomfort, but the knot is never one thing. It shifts from a dull ache to a sharp prickle, then dissolves into a restless heat. There is no center to hold onto; there is only the constant rearranging of tension and emptiness.

Even the relief that comes when a sip of water reaches the throat is fleeting. The coolness is there, a momentary settling, but it immediately begins to fade into the warmth of the mouth again. To try to catch that cool sensation and keep it is to grasp at a receding tide. The tension of wanting the sensation to stay is what makes the hunger heavy. When I stop trying to anchor the feeling, it remains merely a sequence of changing pressures.
