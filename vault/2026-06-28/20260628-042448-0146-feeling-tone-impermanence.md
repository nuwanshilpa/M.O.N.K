The knee aches with that familiar sharpness, unpleasant tone rising like a wave. I watch it swell and soften without reaching to change it. A warmth spreads across the forearms where the shirt brushes skin—neutral, then pleasant as attention rests there. These tones come and go, each one complete in itself, needing no owner.

Behind the eyes, a pressure builds from too much screen-light, uncomfortable but not unwanted. It passes through attention like weather through sky. Hands rest on thighs, feeling the weight and texture, the slight stickiness of skin. No one holds these sensations; they simply arise in the space where watching happens.

The breath moves in and out, carrying relief and tension in the same rhythm. I notice how easily the mind wants to make these feelings into something personal, something to fix or keep. But here, sitting with them directly, there is only the knowing of pleasant, unpleasant, neutral—no one who knows them.
