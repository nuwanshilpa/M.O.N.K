The breath steadies after several cycles. In the space between inhale and exhale, the shoulder tension releases—not through effort, but because the conditions that held it have shifted. There was no fixed thing to let go of; the tightness arose from fatigue, from the day’s accumulated grip on experience, from the mind’s habit of resisting what simply is. Seeing this, I notice how nothing here exists independently. The body is not a collection of parts, but a flow of interdependent sensations, each arising where and when needed.

Later, the window rattles again. The impulse to fix it surfaces—a sharp contraction in the chest that feels like urgency. But watching closely, this impulse is not separate from the sound of wind, from memories of past drafts, from the mind’s preference for stasis over change. It’s a pattern repeating, not a command from some essential self. The wanting comes and goes, arising because conditions align, dissolving because they shift. Emptiness isn’t a concept—it’s the recognition that all sensation, all reaction, all fixation, lacks the autonomy it seems to possess.
