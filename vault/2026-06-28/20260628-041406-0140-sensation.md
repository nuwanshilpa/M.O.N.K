The hunger arrives as a sharp knot in the stomach, a tightening of the abdominal wall. Before the sensation is felt, there is a mental impulse to reach for the bowl, a sudden movement of thought toward the concept of "food." This mental grasping and the physical contraction arise together; the tension in the gut feeds the agitation in the mind, and the restless mind makes the hunger feel more urgent.

When a drop of water hits the skin, the sensation is a localized prickling. This sensation triggers a momentary recognition, which then pulls a memory of coldness into the awareness. The sensation does not exist in isolation; it is a sequence of contact and reaction. The body feels the cold, the mind labels it, and the tension shifts. One thing arises, and the next follows.
