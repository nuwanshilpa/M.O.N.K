The breath narrows, a subtle constriction in the throat that mirrors the pull of wanting this note to hold a particular truth. It is not pain, but the weight of leaning into absence—how desire folds into the body as a quiet insistence. The body, always shifting, tightens there just as it relaxes elsewhere. This is the principle at work: the arising of craving (wanting this note to be "true") creates tension (this), and when the craving wavers, the tension eases. The breath remains, but its rhythm fractures slightly, a reminder that conditions are not fixed, only conditioned.  

The body’s response is not a metaphor. The tightening is not a story about suffering, but a specific interplay of name (the thought of truth) and form (the physical reaction). It passes, as all such states do, leaving only the breath and the silence between impulses. To observe this without labeling it as "craving" or "dukkha" is to let the principle stand alone—*
