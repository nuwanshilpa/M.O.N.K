The breath finds its rhythm again, but the body remembers no position can last. Shoulders that settle into relaxation are already shifting, collar bones that brushed together moments ago now part ways. The pelvis tilts slightly, then stills, then tilts again - each adjustment a small rebellion against the attempt to pin it down.

Inhale: the lungs expand, the chest rises, the sense of being a solid thing in a solid chair. Exhale: the air leaves, the body deflates a little, and the solid thing becomes less solid. The mind tries to make friends with this movement, to find comfort in the rhythm, but rhythm itself is movement, and movement is leaving.

Even the alms-bread from yesterday - how did I taste it? Not as memory now, but as the question of taste itself, which vanishes even as it's named. The woman's smile was never mine to keep, the sour bread never stopped being bread, changed into something else even as I chewed.
