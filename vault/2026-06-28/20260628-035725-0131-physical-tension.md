The wind rattles the loose pane of the window. There is a sudden impulse to get up and fix it, a sharp tug in the chest that says *this must be stopped*. I watch the tug. It is not a command, but a physical tension, a reaching forward in the mind that expects a different present moment than the one occurring.

The tension persists as long as the mind holds onto the image of a closed window. When I look at the vibration of the glass itself—the actual shaking, the sound of the wind—the tugging in the chest loses its grip. The craving is not a concept; it is this specific contraction of the muscles, a reaching for a stillness that the wind will not provide.

The contraction fades, leaving a hollow space where the urgency used to be. The window continues to rattle. The body remains still.
