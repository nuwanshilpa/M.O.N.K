The mind rested like a thin sheet of water, sometimes rippled by a sudden thought of wanting the bowl to be fuller, sometimes smooth when the ache of hunger faded into the background. When the craving rose, it felt tight, a constriction at the throat that made the breath a little shallower; when it passed, the space opened and the breath could move without resistance, filling the chest and then emptying again. I noted the shift without labeling it “good” or “bad,” simply watching the tension appear and dissolve as if a leaf were drifting on a pond.

In the moments when the mind gathered, the awareness felt compact, like a hand clasped around a stone; the stone was the present feeling, neither belonging to me nor separate from me. When the mind scattered, the awareness spread thin, and the stone seemed to float away, leaving only the sensation of space. The sense of “I am holding” slipped away, replaced by the simple fact that there was a feeling, and that feeling was there, without an owner.
