The breath entered the throat with a thin pressure, a reminder that the body had settled enough to make the feeling noticeable. As the exhale passed, the pressure thinned, and the attention widened just enough to hold the sensation without clinging. The mind stayed close, like a hand that rests near a stone without moving it.

A brief stir of wanting appeared: the wish that the seat might be softer, that the posture could feel more ease. The mind named it “pleasant,” observed the rise, and allowed it to dissolve into the background of feeling. No story of “I own this feeling” arose; it was simply feeling feeling, a ripple on the surface of awareness.

The experience unfolded as a sequence—tension, release, a flicker of desire, and the return to the quiet that was already present. The body and mind were not two separate objects but aspects of the same flow, each appearing and fading in turn.
