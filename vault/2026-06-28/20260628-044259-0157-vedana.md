The breath was uneven, sharp at the edges. It entered and left like a demand, not an invitation. The body breathed, but not freely—each exhale faltered slightly, as though resisting what must come next. The skin felt tight, not with pain, but with a quiet insistence, like a membrane stretched thin between softness and restraint.  

The feeling-tone was not fixed. It shifted between subtle pressure and a haze of dullness, like mist clinging to glass. It moved from the throat down to the shoulders, then lifted into the chest, where it settled like a weight that was not a burden. There was no one to name it, only the movement of sensation itself—arising, lingering, dissolving.  

The body was not separate from this. It was not a vessel, but the field. Breath moved freely within, and the marks of impermanence and non-self were not abstract—they were here, in the rise and fall, in the brief stillness between inhale and exhale. The tension loosened just enough to show what it was.
