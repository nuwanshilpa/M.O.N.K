The claim that "what cannot be kept cannot give lasting rest" feels too neat now, like a doorway I thought I understood but was actually avoiding the thornier parts. Sitting with the afternoon light filtering through leaves, I notice how even this moment of apparent peace carries its own edge - the body's weight, the mind's chatter, the slow fade of concentration. Dissatisfaction isn't just about loss; it's in the very texture of staying. Pleasant sensations don't escape dukkha either - they pull us toward wanting more, or toward resisting their end. The teaching stands, but my grasping at it as a stable principle was itself a kind of clinging.
