A crow calls outside the window, and the ear meets the sound in a moment that feels like a small collision. The hearing arises, and with it a flicker of memory—my mother's garden, the same bird years ago. The memory brings a warmth that tightens into something like longing.  

When the call stops, the tightening loosens on its own. No effort needed, only the natural fading that comes when contact ends. If I trace the sequence: the sound ceased, the feeling faded, the story dissolved. There is no grip here, only the clear seeing of how things pass when their conditions fall away.
