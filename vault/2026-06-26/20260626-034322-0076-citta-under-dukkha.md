The warmth tightens into something almost possessive, this memory of crows and mother's garden, as if the mind grasps to keep it from slipping away. But the holding only makes the space around it tighter, more pressure. The sound has faded, yet the echo remains, demanding continuation.

I try to let the memory be, but it pulls at the edges of awareness like a thread through cloth. The more I focus on not-clinging, the more the clinging announces itself—loud as the crow, quiet as breath. The mind cannot contain what was never its to keep.
