The breath draws in, and with it a sensation of warmth spreads across the chest. The warmth is present, and at the same instant the throat tightens. The tightening feels like a pull that tries to keep the warmth from slipping away. The tightening itself is felt, and the mind registers it as a subtle urge: “Let this stay.”

When the warmth ceases, the tightening fades. The moment the breath exhales, the throat loosens and the sense of pulling dissolves. The mind’s notice of the tightening is not a thought about a distant future but an immediate reaction to the body’s shift. Each rise or fall of the breath happens with a series of sensations that arise and cease together, forming a chain that repeats itself without any external command.
