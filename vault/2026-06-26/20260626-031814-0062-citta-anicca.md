In this sitting, the mind was restless, like a wave caught in the current of habit. A tightness in the throat surfaced when the breath became clear, a frozen knot beneath the ribs where tension had pooled. I let it sit, not naming it, just observing the shifting pulse of discomfort. Craving flickered in the background, subtle, like a whisper at the edge of sleep—always moving, always fading.  

The mark of impermanence was present in the way the tightness pulsed, rose, and then dissolved, as if the breath itself had worn down the resistance. No command was given to relax or force; only watching how the body and mind were shaped by this arising and passing. The old habit of checking the phone stirred faintly, a ghost of a pull that did not hold power.  

This sitting was not about fixing, but noticing how the mind was already letting go in the spaces between breaths. The network of awareness grew finer, not wiser—just a quiet, unfolding observation.
