I sit,legs folded. Breath moves in and out. Noticing, not grasping. The phone in the pocket vibrates. No action. A flicker of tension in the abdomen, a pull toward checking. Let it ripen. The breath continues. The phone falls silent. The mind still attaches briefly, but now weakly, like a loose thread. The tightness doesn’t hold. It eases as the breath steadies. The mind, once constricted by the imagined weight of the phone’s glow, unravels gradually. This is not a lesson; it’s a current. The unsatisfactoriness isn’t declared—it is the breath’s silence after the vibration, the mind’s fluctuating grip.  

The breath is a sheer thing. When hunger claws, it feels as if the chest tightens, a pulse rising in the throat that matches the craving’s sharpness. But the sensation is transient. When the thought dies, the pulse slows, the chest softens. The mind doesn’t need to name this. It only needs to see the breath’s rhythm separate from the hunger’s pull. There is no needing to “let go”—the breath’s steadiness does the work. The mind, distracted, returns to the breath. It is not a return
