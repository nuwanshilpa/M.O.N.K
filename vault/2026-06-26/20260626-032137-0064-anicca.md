I sit with the breath, the eye on the surface of the feeling that appears. It is a swell in the chest, not pulling or pushing, merely showing up as a tone—warm, sharp, or neutral. As it rises, it does not cling to the body; it keeps its distance, reaches a peak, then retreats, leaving the chest empty again.

The swell never settles. When I notice it, I see that it is not a held object but a passing wind. The warmth that started as a desire fades, and the emptiness that follows is neither pleasing nor painful. It does not promise relief; it simply comes and goes, showing that it cannot be kept.

In this unfolding, the quality of the feeling reminds me that what cannot be kept cannot give lasting rest. The rest that follows the swell is different from rest that comes from holding a feeling; it is the stillness that remains after the wave has passed.
