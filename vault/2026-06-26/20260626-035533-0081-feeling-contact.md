The contact of fingertip to the cushion is a quiet tremor that wakes before the thought that it might be a trembling. The trembling is felt as a subtle vibration in the palm, a slight pull that follows the breath. When the breath deepens, the vibration flattens; when the breath lightens, it sharpens. The mind stays an observer, noting the rise and fall as a simple if‑then chain: contact gives vibration, vibration gives a feeling of discomfort, discomfort gives the urge to ease it by shifting weight. The urge is a ripple that carries the pattern of from‑to‑from, from‑to‑to.

When the urge is left unattended, it ceases. The vibration subsides, the discomfort dissolves, the mind returns to the simply noted pulse of breath. The pattern repeats: contact again, vibration again, urge again, and eventually a silence of the urge itself. Each cycle is a small map of dependence: feeling arises from contact, feeling gives a mental reaction, mental reaction can produce a physical movement or a pause.

The body is the mirror of this chain. The knee flexes only after the mind has triggered a movement. The breath slows only after the mind has lifted the sensation of lightness. Nothing stays fixed, every link lengthens or shortens with the next breath, with the next thought. Even the silence is a link, a pause that is still a state of feeling awaiting the next contact.
