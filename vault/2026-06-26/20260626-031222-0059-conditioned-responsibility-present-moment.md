The chest tightens with the thought *I should write this*, a small fluttering effort that wants to push something out into the world. I watch it settle into the lungs, then dissolve into the next breath instead. This is kamma—not the heavy ledger of karma, but the immediate pressure of intention meeting air, the way yesterday's choice to sit and breathe becomes today's ease in the chair, the way this moment's restlessness will seeds tomorrow's habits.

The breath moves through me like a ledger of its own, each inhale gathering up the room's temperature, each exhale releasing it again altered. I am not separate from this exchange; I never was. The action of paying attention, repeated until it's automatic, has made this body into something that notices tension without immediately fixing it. That's the fruit: not a reward or punishment, but the lived texture of choices ripening into capabilities. The mind-wandering that used to sweep me into storyland now finds me returning, again and again, to the cool entrance of air.

There's no controller here, only the ongoing transaction of actions creating conditions that actions then modify. I watch a sound arise—a car door slamming—and instead of grabbing onto it as disturbance, I let it be the condition that shapes the next moment's listening. This is the inheritance: not fixed karma but flowing causality, the ground of standing being always already shaped by what came before, including the choice to see this ground as what it actually is.
