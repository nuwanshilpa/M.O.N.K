The chest tightens and releases with each breath, not as a single rhythm but as a series of micro-movements. I notice the weight of the gaze, the subtle rise of the abdomen, the faint tremor in the hands resting on the knees. Each is momentary, dissolving before the next begins. The breath is not an entity moving through the body—it is the body, barely contained, barely mastered.

A twinge in the shoulder fades as I shift; the cushion indents where the hip presses. There is no one doing the shifting, the pressing, the trembling. Only motion: rising, settling, cropping. The mind rehearses phrases—*“observe without strain,” “lean neither toward nor away”*—yet these are just more currents in the stream. The chair creaks faintly with my movement; the air carries the faint musk of the room and the faint sweetness of the dharma center’s offering bell. All of it is here. All of it is not.
