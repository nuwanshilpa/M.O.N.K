The urge for more rice shows up as a faint pressure behind the eyes, a tightening of the jaw that feels like a band rather than a command. I let the sensation stay, noticing its texture without trying to change it or follow it.

On the damp path, each step presses the sole into soft loam; the grains shift and re‑form under weight, and a brief shadow pools beneath the foot before the moisture spreads and disappears. The ground cannot hold a steady comfort; it yields and moves with every shift of weight.

Both the craving band and the shifting soil reveal a quality that cannot be kept and therefore cannot give lasting rest. The experience itself shows the unsatisfactoriness directly, without needing to add any interpretation.
