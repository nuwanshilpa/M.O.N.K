The breath comes in cool at the nostrils, a thin line of air that slides down the throat and expands the chest. Each inhalation ends with a faint pause, then the exhale releases a warm current that brushes the upper lip before fading. The rhythm is not forced; it simply appears and disappears, each cycle a brief event without a holder.

The body rests on the cushion, weight pressing through the sit bones into the floor. Knees settle outward, hands rest on the thighs, palms open. A subtle sway arises when the breath deepens, a micro‑movement that travels from the spine to the shoulders, then settles again. The contact points — feet, hips, hands — feel solid, then soften, then solid once more.

Heat rises in the belly as the breath fuels a quiet fire; moisture gathers at the corners of the eyes, a thin film of water. The breath itself is wind, moving through passages that are neither owned nor directed. In each sensation — cool, warm, pressure, release — there is no separate “I” that claims it; the elements simply arise and pass, known only as they appear.
