I sit on the mat, the coolness lingering from the night. The breath traces a small line at the tip of the nose, and behind it a tug in the throat that reminds me I want to reach for my phone. I notice it as a shift in the body, not as a command.

That tug is a thought that appears, stays for a moment, and then fades. There is nothing that owns it; it simply comes into the awareness and leaves. The sense of an “I” that could own the tug never surfaces – the label slips away before it can root.

The breath continues, a simple rise and fall, and the tug dissolves. Each is a temporary assembly of sensations, nothing that persists as a self or a mine.
