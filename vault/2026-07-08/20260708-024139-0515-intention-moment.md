The finger hovers over the key. A tiny surge — *delete, rewrite, say it cleaner* — rises before the word forms. That surge is the action; the keystroke is only its shadow. The sentence appears, and with it a faint tightening in the chest: *good enough* or *not quite*. The fruit is already ripening in the same breath that planted it.

Later, walking the corridor, a colleague's voice carries irritation. The mind drafts a reply — sharp, justified — before the feet have stopped. The draft is the deed; the spoken words are just the echo. The jaw stays clenched for minutes after. The seed was the flash of righteousness; the fruit is this heat in the face, this narrowing of the next hour.

Even the wish to "see kamma clearly" arrives as a movement, not a view. It pulls the attention toward the next moment's choice, coloring it with expectation. The contour of that coloring shapes what meets the eye. No separate watcher stands outside the chain — only link touching link, each one both fruit and seed.
