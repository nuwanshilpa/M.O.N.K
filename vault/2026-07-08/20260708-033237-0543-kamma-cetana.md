I notice the intention to lift a foot before the movement begins — a subtle tightening in the hip, a quiet decision that precedes the lift. The foot rises, the weight shifts, and the body feels the ground release its hold for a moment.  

After the foot settles again, there is a lingering sensation in the sole, a faint pressure that remains where the foot touched. The stance feels slightly different, as if the surface has been subtly altered by that contact, and the next step begins from that altered base.  

Over several steps, the pattern repeats: each movement leaves a tiny imprint that conditions the next lift, not as a rule I accept but as a felt continuity — the way the body stands is constantly shaped by the very actions it has just performed.
