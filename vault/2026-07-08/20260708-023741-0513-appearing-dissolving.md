The tea cup sits warm in my hands, but the warmth is already moving, spreading thin across the ceramic surface. I watch the steam rise and dissolve into the air, each wisp appearing complete then vanishing into what was already there. This is how it feels - not that things lack existence, but that they do not grasp onto it themselves. The cup holds its shape only as long as I do not look too closely at its edges, where light meets shadow and certainty falters.

A sound comes from the street below, sharp and sudden. It arrives fully formed, then empties itself into silence. There was never anything solid there to begin with - just vibration meeting ear, interpretation arising in the gap between stimulus and response. The meaning I give it tries to fill that space, but the space remains larger than whatever story I tell about the car backfiring, or door slamming, or whatever it was. Looking directly at this, I see how each moment opens like this - not barren, but generous, holding what appears without needing to keep it.
