The feeling‑tone came unbidden, a subtle warmth that settled in the chest and then a quick prick of sharpness at the temples. It rose, lingered briefly, then faded without any urge to grasp or push away. In the space between each shift there was just the plain sensation, untouched by thought, and the body remained steady, the chair’s support unnoticed yet present.

As I kept turning my attention to the tone, the sense of a separate self slipped away. There was no “I” feeling the warmth, no owner of the sharp edge, only the direct experience itself. The memory of earlier sittings provided a gentle background, a condition that reminded rather than constrained, allowing the present moment to appear on its own.
