I sit with the breath, the air moving in and out, and a faint throb in the knee rises with each inhale, softens with each exhale, then fades only to return a moment later. The tone of that sensation is neither welcome nor unwelcome; it simply appears, lingers, and dissolves, and I watch it without reaching for a story or a label. The body’s signal is a quiet pulse that comes and goes, a small ripple in the flow of the present.

When the mind reaches out, trying to name the throb as pain or as a sign of something else, the sensation shifts—its quality changes, its intensity wanes, and a new subtle feeling replaces it. The attempt to grasp creates a subtle tension, and the feeling mutates, showing how quickly anything conditioned changes when attention is drawn to it. In this change I see directly that nothing remains still.

The observation settles into a gentle openness: I am not the holder of the throb, nor its avoider, simply the space in which it arises and passes. The note of this sitting is the simple seeing of impermanence, felt in the knee’s modest pulse.
