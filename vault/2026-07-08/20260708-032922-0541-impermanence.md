The breath arrives, and with it consciousness of the breath - not separate from it, not located anywhere, just the knowing that this movement is happening. Inhale: consciousness knows coolness at the nostrils, expansion in the chest. Exhale: consciousness knows warmth, settling, the slight pause between cycles. But this knowing isn't held by anything - there's no owner of this awareness, no watcher behind the eyes. Just the knowing-arising-with-the-breath, and the breath-arising-with-the-knowing. When the breath stops momentarily, consciousness doesn't pursue it; it simply doesn't arise. Then it returns with the next breath, identical yet new.

Consciousness here is not a thing that owns the breathing; it's the very brightness that illuminates the process from within. The chest doesn't rise because consciousness commands it - both arise together, linked by conditions. The sense of "me" breathing dissolves not through effort but through attention: seeing how the one who supposedly breathes is itself breathed, seen, known. The spine straightens not because Will decides, but because leaning back against the cushion creates conditions, and consciousness arises with that experience too. Each moment of knowing includes the knowing of impermanence - everything here is subject to ceasing, including this very awareness.

The pattern becomes clear through repetition: this consciousness, these breaths, this body - all arise interdependent, all cease interdependently. No isolated self finds itself observing this process; only the process observes itself, briefly, clearly, without ownership. links: impermanence, non-self
