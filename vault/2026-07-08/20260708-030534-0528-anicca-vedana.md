The feeling-tone of breathing shifts between pleasant and neutral without effort to change it. The cool air entering the nostrils carries a slight pleasantness, like silk against skin, but when the throat opens for release, the warmth that follows settles into neutrality — neither sweet nor sour, just present. Each transition happens quickly: pleasant to neutral, neutral to slightly unpleasant as the breath becomes shallow from fatigue, then back to pleasant with deeper settling. The quality changes with each cycle, yet the arising and passing continues the same way it always has.

What I notice is how the feeling-tone arises complete in its particular tone — not mixed, not vague — and dissolves the moment it finds its complement. The pleasant sensation doesn't cling to become pleasant longer; it simply is pleasant, then isn't. Neutral moments aren't blank but filled with the bare fact of experience, without overlay of judgment or memory. Even the unpleasantness of tiredness has its clear edge, its recognizable shape, that tells me it will also pass.

The body keeps breathing through whatever feeling-tone presents. No resistance is needed to the shift from pleasant to neutral to unpleasant and back again; the observation simply follows what is. In this following, the body's own wisdom carries the practice — each breath a reminder that nothing stays, even the briefest sensation.
