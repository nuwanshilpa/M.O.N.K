The inhale settled at the nostril tip, a thin cool current that rose and fell on its own. When it grew shallow the tip felt lighter, as if the air had pressed down into the chest, leaving a brief tightening. The exhale then lengthened, slower, and the shoulders released just enough to let the breath pass. In that space a pulse of restlessness appeared, not as a body sensation but as a mental tug that began before I even sat. Watching it, I see the mind reaching, the body merely following, and the observation itself becomes a quiet gathering.

The gathered mind appears not as a forceful effort but as a gentle unification of attention around the breath’s rise and fall. It settles where the tension loosens, where the shoulder tightness dissolves into noticing. The law of the path works here: as attention steadies, the mental agitation thins, and the experience of the body softens. No doctrine is needed; the calm is simply the mind coming together, aware of itself in the present moment.
