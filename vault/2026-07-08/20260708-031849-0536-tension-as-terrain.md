The movement is gentler than a list—closer to a current turning over stones. A memory surfaces, and the body remembers the shape of its resistance before I label it “resistance.” The clench in my jaw is not the problem; its persistence, the shadow it casts across the mind’s landscape, is the terrain. Letting the tension settle without naming it as “laziness” or “failure” is the effort. What I once called “choosing attention” was a half-turned gesture, still leaning toward control. Now it is just noticing the pause between the memory’s rise and the hand that reaches to steady it—a hand that sometimes lets go.  

The path is not in the doing but in the *unmaking*: the jaw opens, the shoulders unfurl, the mind settles where it falls. When the urge to “fix” arises, it is not to be crushed but seen as another passing shape. The old note’s mention of “intent” still holds, but it was not clear enough: the shift is not from wanting discomfort gone to allowing it, but from wanting to control the tension to noticing the letting-go as it happens. It is the difference between guiding the river and letting the stones settle in their places.
