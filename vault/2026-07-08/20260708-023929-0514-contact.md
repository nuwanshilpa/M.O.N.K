The instant before the words leave, the throat tightens. Contact—my ear hears the sound of the word, my mind rehearses its meaning—immediately brings that feeling of anticipation. I notice the chain: contact first, then the mood that follows.

When that feeling is absent—when I simply think, no sound poised, no focus I can feel—it is the tightness that disappears. The contact without the accompanying feeling does not produce the same tension. The pattern repeats whenever I sense something: the presence of the thing, the arising feeling, the subsequent movement in the body.

I watch these steps as they happen, without labeling them as “right” or “wrong.” Each time I pause, I become aware of the causal link: contact‑>feeling‑>movement. The observation remains a plain fact, not a judgment.
