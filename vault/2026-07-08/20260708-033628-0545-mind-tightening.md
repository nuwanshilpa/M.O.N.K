The mind feels taut this morning, drawn tight around a low hum of wanting — coffee, quiet, the day not to begin yet. Each time the breath returns, the grip loosens a fraction, then tightens again around the next thought. No decision to release, no effort to hold; the tightening and loosening simply happen, like fists opening and closing in sleep.

A sound outside — a door, a bird — and attention snaps toward it, then drifts back. The mind is not scattered so much as flicked, again and again, by whatever arises. There is no center holding it together, only the repeated return. Each return feels different: sometimes sharp, sometimes dull, sometimes already gone before it's noticed.
