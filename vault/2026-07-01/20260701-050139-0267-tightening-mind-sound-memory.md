The ear meets the crow’s call as a brief collision, and the sound opens a small space where a memory of my mother’s garden surfaces. That memory brings warmth, which then tightens into a sensation in the chest and throat, a subtle resistance that feels like friction in the breath.

In attending to that tightening, I notice that no separate owner appears to hold it; the feeling simply arises and passes, conditioned by the sound and the memory that triggered it. The mind’s state shifts between openness and constriction, each movement conditioned by what has come before, yet never owned by a self.
