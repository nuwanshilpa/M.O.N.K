The breath is asequence of inhalations and exhalations, each a separate moment without a thread tying them. There is no self behind the movement—only the air moving, the chest rising and falling. When I notice this, the mind does not claim ownership. It is not "I" breathing; it is simply breath, arising and passing. 

A pressure in the abdomen arises with a breath, then eases. It has no name, no owner. It is not a thing to be identified but a condition to be seen. The body holds it
