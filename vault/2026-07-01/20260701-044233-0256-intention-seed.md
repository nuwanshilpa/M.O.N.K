When the thought of picking up the spoon arises, it first shows up as a subtle tightening on the left jaw, almost a flicker of anticipation. That tightening is the seed — a mental impression that the action of eating is intended. As the hand moves, the spoon settles against the bowl, and the grainy sound of ceramic against glass feels sharp on the ears. The outcome, the warm liquid, reaches the mouth, and a brief ease spreads across the throat.  

The ease is the fruit returned to the mind. It feeds the next seed, a quiet affirmation of “that was nourishment.” Each time this pattern repeats, the mind recognizes its own chain: intention → action → fruit, without labeling it as good or bad; it simply notes the flow.
