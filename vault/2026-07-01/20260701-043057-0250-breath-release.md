The breath entered the nostrils as a cool rush, then left as a warm sigh; each inhalation set a slight lift in the chest, each exhalation let the shoulders settle a fraction more. The chair held the weight of the legs, the floor met the soles, and the spine, relaxed, bore the subtle shift of weight as the body adjusted without effort.

In the pause after the exhalation a thin line of tension lingered in the throat, then softened as the breath left, and the mind followed that release, noting the easing without turning it into a fixed point. The quiet that followed the softening was not a belief but a felt result of the preceding movement, a small ripple of kamma in the present moment.
