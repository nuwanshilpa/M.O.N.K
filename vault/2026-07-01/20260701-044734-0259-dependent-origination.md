The tightness in the chest settles, a small weight between the ribs. It arose with the memory of that crow, dependent on sound and association, and it will pass with attention. No solid thing there—only conditions conspiring to create the sensation of tension. The breath continues beneath it, steady and unbound, carrying the body's weight in the chair, the chair's pressure resting on floorboards, the floor holding earth that once was mountain and forest and will be something else. Each layer supports the next without possessing any fixed essence.

Even this writing is empty—letters forming words forming thoughts, all flowing from the intention to respond. The mind that notices the tightness is itself empty, arising when awareness turns inward, ceasing when attention wanders. No controller behind the experience, no witness that is substantial. Just this contingent cascade of causes and effects, each link dependent on the next, none able to claim ownership of the whole.

The emptiness isn't an absence of form or feeling, but the recognition that form and feeling lack the essence they seem to possess. The crow's call echoes in the space between hearing and silence, in the gap where memory meets present sound, in the pause between one breath and the next. Everything is already free, already empty—it appears when we stop grasping for what isn't there.
