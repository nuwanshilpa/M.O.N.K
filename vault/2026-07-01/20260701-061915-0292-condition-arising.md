The exhale completed itself, a soft release that left the body humming with its own absence. In that aftermath, before the next inhale gathered its purpose, I felt the conditions shift—not a void, but a subtle reconfiguration. The lungs rested in their emptiness, the ribs softened, and attention naturally settled into the space between doing and not-doing. Then, without command, the diaphragm engaged, drawing the next breath inward as if responding to the previous state's completion.

I watched this pattern repeat: each arising breath conditioned by the ceasing of the last, each pause a moment where nothing and everything coexisted. The cool slip of air entering followed the warm sigh of leaving, not as separate events but as linked emergences. When the body was tired, the breath shortened; when alert, it deepened—each quality giving rise to the next without fixed owner or controller. The teaching revealed itself not in doctrine but in this ongoing choreography of cause and effect, moment yielding to moment with unforced precision.
