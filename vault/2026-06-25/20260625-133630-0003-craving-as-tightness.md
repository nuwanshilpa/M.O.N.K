The breath slips in and out, and with each inhalation I notice the subtle pull that rises when the mind begins to cling to the feeling of comfort—“I would like this warmth to stay.” The pull is not a thought about the future, it is a sensation in the throat, a tightening that tightens further the longer I allow it to linger. I label it “craving” and keep the attention on the sensation itself, watching how it expands, how the body responds with a slight shift of weight, and how, when I let the label rest, the tightness gradually softens and the breath resumes its plain rhythm.

In the same stretch, a feeling of dissatisfaction appears as I note the rising wish for the breath to be smoother, for the body to be still. That wish is another shape of the same craving, pointing toward a desire that the present moment be different. I observe the feeling, note its beginning, its holding, and its fading, seeing how the yearning itself creates a small knot of tension that later releases when I simply note it without trying to fulfill it. The knot’s opening shows the truth that the discomfort does not arise from the breath itself but from the mind’s reaching for something it cannot hold.

The second noble truth becomes visible in the way the tension eases once the note is placed: the relief is not a permanent peace but a temporary easing of the immediate craving. I notice the ease as it settles, noticing that it too passes, leaving a quiet space where the mind is no longer pulled by that particular urge. The pattern repeats, each cycle showing the way craving births the feeling of suffering, and the simple act of noting offers a brief release.
