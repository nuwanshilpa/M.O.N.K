Thethought of needing more rice appears as a faint pressure behind the eyes, a quiet urge that tightens the jaw. I let the sensation sit, noticing it as a tight band rather than a command to act.  

When I keep the attention on the band without adding story or resistance, the pressure softens after a few breaths; the accompanying unease in the chest eases, and the mind feels lighter, as if a small weight has been lifted.  

This cycle of urge‑tightening‑release repeats with each passing thought of want, showing the fading of craving and the accompanying easing of dukkha as something observable in the moment, not a doctrine to accept.
