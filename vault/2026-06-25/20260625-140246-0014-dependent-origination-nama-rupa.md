The throattightens again as I hold the breath long. Not because I’m controlling it, but because a want settles there—want for ease, for a room, for a thought to not shift. When this want is, a tightness is. When it isn’t, the throat loosens. The breath moves through the same moment: the want’s name, the form of tightness. There is no separate “I” wanting; the want and the tightness arise together. The mind names the want as “to stay warm,” and the body tightens in response. The name and form depend on each other, neither arising alone.
