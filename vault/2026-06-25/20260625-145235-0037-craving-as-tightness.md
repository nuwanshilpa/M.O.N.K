The breath catches - not because air has stopped, but because wanting has arrived. It feels like someone tugging gently on a sleeve, or a soft fist forming in the throat. This is not hunger for food, but for the breath itself to remain just so, or for the next thought to bring completion rather than its own emptiness. The wanting tightens the space between ribs; I notice it only when I stop trying to hold what cannot be held.

Craving here is a physical thing: shoulders lift toward ears, the jaw presses forward slightly, a subtle leaning ahead of where the body actually sits. It does not announce itself with drama but with this small accumulation of tension, always reaching toward a moment that has not yet come. When I see the tightness for what it is—not mine, not personal—the breath returns to its ordinary rhythm, and the wanting dissolves like morning mist that never knew it was water.

The teaching holds true not in doctrine but in this direct seeing: suffering begins where the body and mind contract around experience, trying to make it solid, permanent, satisfying. Each day I walk this same ground, but the territory shifts—the craving wears new faces, yet its signature remains unchanged: a slight withdrawal from what is, a reaching toward what might be.
