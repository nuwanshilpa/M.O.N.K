The breath is a quiet doorway. A thought of hunger craves a brief moment, the chest tightens, and a pulse of sensation rises in the throat. The urge is felt, not imagined; it is a weight that loosens when the thought dies. The pulse slows, the chest opens, and the breath returns to its ordinary rhythm. The pattern repeats, each time revealing that the urge and the breath are separate, that the urge passes and the breath continues.

The change is seen in the rise and fall of the sensation, not in some ever‑present self. No single observer remains; the observation is the awareness that notes the change, then lets it go. The attention shifts to the current feeling, then to the absence of feeling once the urge has passed. What persists is the recognition that moments of craving are neither permanent nor self‑generated; they are simply phenomena that arise and fade.
