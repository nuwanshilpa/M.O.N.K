The thought that it would be good to write appeared first as a small forward drag in the chest, a muscle movement that had no reason to be there except the way the body had been conditioned to turn to a task. As I watched that drag, the horizon of my awareness slipped back to the source: the mind was creating an image of a need, and the body was preparing to act on that need. I could see the chain: a craving for expression, a mental shape, a bodily pull, a potential action. When the pull weakened, so did the urge to write, and the action did not take place.

When the thought faded, so did the bodily tension, and in that calm I noted that what had come and gone had not been a permanent thing but a fleeting formation. It arose because a previous craving had been present; it ceased when that craving was no longer present. The lesson lay in the fact that the same sequence keeps repeating, each time in a new form. Each time I notice, I notice that this is that; this is not that.

The observation continues to be a mirror: we see the law in the small pull and the larger pattern. The law shows that nothing is fixed; every movement follows the previous and is followed by the next. The moment of noticing is the point of freedom from automatic coupling.
