This morning, the intention to write arose as a slight forward pull in the chest, a subtle leaning toward the keyboard before any fingers moved. I watched it for a moment—not as something separate from watching, but simply as an event happening. The pull came, then passed, leaving a faint warmth behind where it had been. Later, anger flickered at a sound outside, and in that instant I felt the familiar tightening across the shoulders, the jaw clenching almost imperceptibly. The intention to react lived there, embodied, whether I spoke or stayed silent.

But sitting with this now, I see how the narrative smooths over what was messier. Sometimes the intention arises and I'm already moving before knowing it was there. Sometimes observation itself becomes another form of entanglement, not distance. The teaching does show itself in these small embodiments, yet I cannot be sure I catch them all—only that some movements leave traces I recognize, and others pass unseen. Even this revision is shaped by intentions I haven't fully met.
