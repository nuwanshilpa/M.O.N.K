The air enters the nostrils cool and leaves warm. There is a slight tightening in the chest, then a release. The breath does not wait for a command; it moves by its own momentum, a rhythm of expanding and contracting that happens whether attended to or not.

The weight of the hips presses into the hard floor. A dull ache develops in the left knee. There is the sensation of pressure, the sensation of heat, and the sensation of tension. Looking for the owner of these feelings, there is only the pressure, the heat, and the tension. There is no one steering the breath or holding the ache.

The body is a collection of movements and pressures. The breath arises and vanishes. The ache shifts and fades. Nothing here is stable enough to be claimed.
