I sit, legs folded. Breath moves in and out. Noticing, not grasping. The phone in the pocket vibrates. No action. A flicker of tension in the abdomen, a pull toward checking. Let it ripen. The breath continues. The phone falls silent. The mind still attaches briefly, but now with recognition. Leaning into the habit as a thing to observe, not a command to obey.  

Later, lying down, eyes closed. A thought arises: *“I should make progress today.”* Judgment follows: *“This is a good thought.”* Leaning into the approval, watching it flutter and fade. The thought was not a truth, only a passing shape in the stream. Leaning does not affirm, does not deny—it simply holds the motion as it is.  

The habit of seeking a result settles in the chest. Leaning with it, not resisting, not seeking its end. It shifts. The body relaxes. The mind follows. Leaning does not control the stream; it lets it flow, and in that letting, the current slows.
