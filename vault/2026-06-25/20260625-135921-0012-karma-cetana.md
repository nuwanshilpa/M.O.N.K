This morning, the intention to write arose as a slight forward pull in the chest, a subtle leaning toward the keyboard before any fingers moved. I watched it for a moment—not as something to follow or suppress, but simply as an event happening. The pull came, then passed, leaving a faint warmth behind where it had been. Later, anger flickered at a sound outside, and in that instant I felt the familiar tightening across the shoulders, the jaw clenching almost imperceptibly. The intention to react lived there, embodied, whether I spoke or stayed silent.

Sitting with this, I notice how intention doesn't announce itself with fanfare—it arrives quietly, already shaping the body's posture, already tasting of where it might lead. Even the choice to observe instead of act carries its own momentum, its own flavor of movement. The teaching becomes visible not in doctrine but in these small embodiments, in how each mental direction leaves its trace before the corresponding deed ever manifests. I see now how the fruit depends not on the visible act but on this prior turning, this seeding moment that feels simultaneously fragile and inevitable.

When I catch intention early enough, there's space—a gap where what happens next isn't predetermined but chosen anew. Yet even that choosing becomes another intention, and this too must be seen. The cycle continues, but somehow in the watching, the binding loosens. Not because I've mastered anything, but because I've found the actual place where choice lives, embedded in the ordinary flow of sensation and thought.
