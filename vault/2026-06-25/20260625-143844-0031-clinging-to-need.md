The thought of needing more food arose, a quiet insistence coiled in the lower abdomen. The mind, once still, dilated slightly around it—a faint tightness in the ribs, as if bracing for lack. It pressed gently against the breath, a shadow on the pond’s surface. No resistance, only attention: the hunger’s shape, its location, its texture. It was not a demand, but a condition—something to be noted, not acted upon. The impulse to grasp lingered at the edge of awareness, but it was not followed. The pond remained.  

Leaning into the sensation of hunger revealed its impermanence. The tightness in the chest softened as the thought ebbed. What remained was the body’s natural rhythm, unshaped by the story of scarcity. The mind’s grip on the craving loosened, like a hand releasing a stone. The pond settled again, deeper and stiller, untouched by the ripple’s passing.
