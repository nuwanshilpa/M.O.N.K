The breath was a single, steady pulse. I felt the coolness at the nostrils when it entered, the warmth when it left. The body was seated in the chair, shoulders slightly fallow, the weight of the legs resting on the floor. The muscles along the spine were relaxed, the belly soft but not flat.

A thought slipped in—“should I move a hand?”—and that was all. The intention hovered at the base of the skull, a faint heat that did not spread. No body part felt pulled to act, no feeling of ownership rooted itself to the gesture. The breath continued its rhythm, indifferent to the flicker of need.

In that simple, unadorned observation, the sense of a separate “me” that might command or urge was absent. The body was present, the breath was present, and I was only the unifying attention that noted them without attaching name or authority. The moment of potential motion dissolved back into breath and stillness.
