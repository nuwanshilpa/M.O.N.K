In the shade of the mango tree the air feels thin, a spaciousness that lets each breath ripple through without crowding. The sensations of sitting arise and dissolve, not as solid possessions but as fleeting appearances within that openness. When I notice a sensation, it is already meeting the empty field that allows it to be seen; there is no need to hold onto the feeling, because the field itself is not a thing to be grasped.

The mind drifts between scattered notes and a quiet gathering, the impulse to write flickering like a candle in the wind. Craving for completion stirs, then softens when attention returns to the simple fact of being present. In this movement the empty space remains unchanged, a backdrop that neither resists nor promotes the rise and fall of thoughts.
