The mind came up as a thin knot in the throat, a pull toward the kitchen that seemed to own the hunger. When I labeled the feeling—“tight,” “craving”—the knot loosened a little, and the thought that followed—“this isn’t mine”—appeared as a separate ripple, quieter than the first urge. I did not try to push the craving away; I simply let the label rest, watching the sensation dissolve into the background of the chair and the air moving in and out of the nostrils.

After the label faded, there was a brief space where nothing was clinging to the feeling. In that gap the mind was neither tight nor open, merely present, like a clear pond after a stone has sunk. No sense of “I own this” arose; the thought and the sensation were just events appearing and passing, each marked by impermanence, each without a holder.
