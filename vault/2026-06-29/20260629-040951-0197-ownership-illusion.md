The searchfor an owner begins quietly — a thought arises, "I am noticing," and attention turns toward the one who notices. But the turning finds only another thought, a faint contraction in the throat, a shift in the visual field. The "I" that seemed to stand behind experience proves to be a label pasted on after the fact, like a name tag on an empty chair. Sensations pulse, sounds arrive and depart, intentions form and dissolve — none of them answer to a manager.

When the mind claims a memory, it feels like possession. Yet the memory is just images and tones reassembling in the present, stitched together by a narrative that wasn't there a moment ago. The body breathes without permission. The heart beats without consultation. Even the intention to investigate anatta arises unbidden, a bubble in the stream. No captain at the helm, only the sea steering itself.
