I sit, legs folded,breath moving in and out. Noticing, not grasping. A phone in the pocket buzzes—a flicker of tension in the abdomen, a pull toward checking. Let it ripen. The breath continues. The phone falls silent. The mind still attaches briefly, but now weakly. The tension ebbs as the breath deepens. The feeling arises, unbidden, then fades. Not by restraint, but by the natural flow of what is sensed. This is not a judgment of the tension or the clinging, but a noting of their arising and passing. They are not permanent, not even lasting a moment.
