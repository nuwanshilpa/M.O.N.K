The breath comes in, shallow at first, as if the body is trying to hold something it cannot grasp. When the inhale reaches the throat I notice a thin thread of longing—an urge to check the phone, to hear the voice that is not there. The thread tightens, not because the phone is absent, but because the mind has already taken the absence as a loss. In that moment the feeling is not the phone itself but the expectation that the phone will bring relief; the expectation is the craving that sets the body into a subtle strain.

As the exhale slips out, the tightness loosens and the body settles back into the floor. The breath continues, indifferent to the story I have woven. The strain fades, and with it the sense that something is missing. The body simply feels the weight of the mat, the coolness of the air, the rhythm of the breath—nothing more, nothing less. The craving that had risen is seen as a ripple that appears and disappears, showing how the first truth moves into the second: the presence of unsatisfied wanting is the very shape of dukkha.

In watching the breath and the fleeting urge without feeding it, the pattern becomes clear: the craving arose, the body reacted, the feeling passed, and the mind was left with a quiet space. That space is the beginning of the third truth, the possibility that the clinging can cease when the pull is observed, not acted upon.
