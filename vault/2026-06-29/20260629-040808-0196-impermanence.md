The sharp heat pulses in the knee, a bright needle of discomfort that demands attention. The mind tenses with the familiar reflex — to fix, to resist, to make it stop. But the sensation has no fixed shape; it flares, softens, fades at the edges, then returns different. I watch the wanting to push it away arise, felt as tension behind the eyes, and how it passes without need for action.

What remains is the steady rhythm of breath, the room's quiet hum, the body's simple presence. The knee's heat was never solid, never mine to hold or lose. The restlessness comes and goes, leaving only this: the breath, the sound of distant traffic, the floor supporting weight without question. There is no need to solve the sensation, only to see it as it is — already changing, already free.
