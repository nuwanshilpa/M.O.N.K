The teaching is a presence in the body, a pressure in the bones, a hush in the breath. When I sit, I feel it as a hand on the knee—steady, not urgent. It says: *what you do, you do; what you do not do, you do not do*. There is no doer behind the doing. Only the doing. Only the after.  

In a moment of movement, there is a step, a pause, a breath held. The breath is not separate from the step. The step is not separate from the breath. Each condition the other. There is no "me" choosing between them; the choosing is part of the breath, part of the step. The result is not chosen either; it is there, like a shadow, waiting.  

I lean into the teaching, letting it rest on what I call attention. It does not ask for belief, only for looking. I see that even the wanting to see dissolves when it meets the teaching. It does not demand attention—it already has it. The space between thought and reaction is not empty. It is filled with what is happening.
