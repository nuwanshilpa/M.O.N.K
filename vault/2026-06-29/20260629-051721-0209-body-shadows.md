The breath comes in, a thin ribbon of air that brushes the nostrils, cool at first, warm at the exit. It passes through the chest like a current, making the ribs hinge and the shoulders lift slightly. I follow that movement, not with a thought of “this must be something,” but with a steady, open watching. The body is a sum of shifting parts: skin feels the draft, muscle trembles, the heart ticks, the mind flutters. None of these parts seem to own the experience; each arises when the next condition appears and disappears when it does not.

I notice that the urge to label – “I am here, I am breathing, I am not” – is itself a fleeting shape, a word that rises on the tongue and falls into the space of the moment. When I let the observation stay, no separate “self” appears to hold the breath or to judge it. The breath is merely a breath; the body a body. The mind, as I see it, is a series of images that arrive, settle, and slip away, like shadows on a wall. There is no fixed owner of these shadows; only the shifting illumined surface where they cast.

In this quiet I feel the continuity of the current, the way the body changes with each pulse of air, and the way the mind moves in tandem without claiming agency. The sense of ‘I’ dissolves into the same fluid that carries the breath, revealing that what we call ‘self’ is nothing more than a passing assembly of conditions.
