The old note spoke of a tightness, a possessive warmth in the memory of a garden. Looking back, that "tightness" was just a specific knot of tension in the chest that has since loosened and reshaped itself. The crow's sound is not an echo demanding continuation; it is simply a vibration that arrived and departed. To say the mind "cannot contain" what it does not own is still a way of making a drama out of a passing weather pattern.

What remains is the friction. When a memory arises, there is a momentary tugging, a brief gathering of heat in the center of the awareness. It is not a struggle against a thread, but a brief, singular sensation of pulling. The pulling does not belong to a person; it is just a sequence of rising and falling pressures. The pressure arrives, it stays for a few breaths, and then the pressure itself dissolves.
