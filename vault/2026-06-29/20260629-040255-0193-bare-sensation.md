Breath moves in and out. No wave, no rhythm — just pressure shifting at the nostrils, the belly rising, falling. Sometimes it's smooth, sometimes jagged. No stillness underneath, only the next breath arriving before the last one finishes.

The body is not a container holding anything. Heat in the knees, pressure where the cushion meets bone, an itch that appears and vanishes without a trace. Each sensation is complete in itself, unrelated to the one before. No pattern, no story of arising and passing — just this heat, this pressure, this itch, gone.

No one watches. The knowing and the known are the same movement. When attention drifts to a sound down the hall, the breath doesn't wait. It continues without a witness.
