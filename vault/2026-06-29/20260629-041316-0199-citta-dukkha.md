Themind sits like a tide pooled at the edge of sand—sometimes restless, sometimes still. When held in attention, it reveals itself as waves of thought or stillness, neither good nor bad, but with a quality of pressure that only grows when noticed. The tightness doesn’t last; it loosens before the next breath arrives, as if the act of clinging to it is what sustains it. There’s a rhythm here: a grip that releases, a grabbing that lets go. Not a cycle of failure, but a pattern that doesn’t need labeling. The mind’s nature isn’t fixed; it’s a thing that moves, however slowly.  

Observing without naming the states allows them to unfold. A moment of wanting arises—not a loud urge, but a subtle pull toward something unseen. It doesn’t demand acknowledgment
