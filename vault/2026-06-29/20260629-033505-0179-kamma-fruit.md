A memory rose—not as story, but as sensation: the sharp pinch of a thrown stone against the shin, the sudden gasp, the way the hand rose instinctively to hold it. Then, before words could spin, the breath caught, the shoulders drew up, and the mind already anticipated pain before it arrived. No story had yet formed, only the immediate pressure in the body, the flicker of recoil. In that gap—between sensation and reaction—the teaching surfaced: not as instruction, but as observation. The action (tossing the stone) and its fruit (the sting) were not chained by time; they were linked by the presence of attention—or its absence. When attention was full, the sting was just sensation, no story. When attention drifted, the story built its scaffold, and the fruit ripened not as memory, but as expectation.

Later, in the same posture, the same pinch returned—this time imagined, not remembered. A phantom wound, tender in the mind’s eye. The body responded before the thought finished: breath shallow, jaw tight. The teaching held its ground not as doctrine, but as terrain: here is the action (thinking the thought), and here is the fruit (the bodily tightening), and between them—no delay, only a shift in attention’s direction. What conditions what is not sequential; it is co-arising, co-ceasing. The knot beneath the ribs is not a flaw in practice, but a signpost pointing to the loop: *this* is bhava, becoming—built breath by breath by thought.
