I sit with the words “the four noble truths” resting in my mind like a quiet stone. The phrase itself is a soft vibration, not a command, and I notice a faint tightening across my shoulders as the thought settles. Breath moves in and out, and the tightness ebbs slightly with each exhale, then returns when the mind turns the phrase over again.

A craving to understand the teaching fully arises, a subtle urge to grasp it as a solution. I feel a light pressure behind the eyes, a wanting to make the idea solid. When I let the urge be just a sensation, without following it into story, the pressure softens and a brief spaciousness appears in the chest. The teaching remains present, not as something to hold, but as a backdrop against which the urge appears and passes.

Later, a faint restlessness flickers in the fingertips, a habit of reaching for completion. I notice it, label it as “wanting to see the end,” and let it sit.
