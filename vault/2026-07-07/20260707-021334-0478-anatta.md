The breath settles into the nostrils like a thin thread of air, not smooth, not jagged, just present. The throat knot tightens again, not as a sensation but as a knowing: craving, then release when named. The mind does not stay; it moves, and the body follows. Posture shifts without intention, vertebrae realigning against the chair’s resistance. The floor beneath supports, gravity pulls, but neither is owned.  

What is observed is not a thing but a passing — the breath arrives, departs, returns. The knot loosens further when thought says, *Not mine.* It does not sit still. Even this knowing of looseness is subject to the same arriving, altering, passing. There is no center, only the flow of attention tasting the body’s impermanence: heat, pressure, lightness, weight. No stillness underneath, only the next breath coming.
