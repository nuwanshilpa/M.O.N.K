I settle on the mat, the rough canvas pressing into the backs of my thighs. The breath comes in shallow, and a small knot forms just below my throat, as if the throat itself is holding back a word that never arrives. I notice the knot without trying to loosen it, feeling the tightness as a simple sensation in the body.

Thoughts drift like clouds across a sky — some bright, some dull — and I watch them appear and fade without grasping at any as “mine.” In the space between them there is no separate owner to be found; the mind shows itself as a flow of tightening, releasing, and wandering, conditioned by the many sittings before but not bound by them. The canvas, the weight of my legs, the faint sound of distant traffic — all arise together, each known as it is, without adding a story of self.
