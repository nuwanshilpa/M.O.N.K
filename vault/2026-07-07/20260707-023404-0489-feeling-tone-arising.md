Afaint coolness touches the tip of the nose as the breath enters — neutral, barely noticed. Then a warmth spreads across the palms, pleasant, and attention rests there a moment longer than needed. An itch flares at the left ankle, sharp and unpleasant, and the urge to scratch rises before the feeling is even named. Each tone arrives, lingers, dissolves. No manager chooses which to follow. The pleasant warmth does not belong to anyone; the unpleasant itch has no owner. They are simply events in the field, known and gone.

The labeling — pleasant, unpleasant, neutral — happens after the fact, a quiet whisper in the wake of the raw tone. When the whisper stops, the tones still come and go. The breath cools the nose. The ankle itches again. The palms cool. Nothing holds them together except the knowing that sees them. That knowing, too, is just another event — noticed, then not.
