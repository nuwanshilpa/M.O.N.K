The breath was a thread running through the tight knot in my chest. It snaked straight to the center, then curled back out, as if pulling itself into a calm. The tightness felt like a small stone lodged just under the ribs. I held the breath, not to force a release but to see what would happen if it moved with its own gravity. The stone did not shiver; it simply settled a touch farther left, a millimetre from the original spot. A quiet acceptance of the shift, no effort, no resistance, just a plain recognition of where the body was.

The craving for more rice, once a roaring fire, had become a faint ember. When I let it sit in the same space as the knot, its heat was no longer a pressure but a mild warmth, like a candle flame that burns but does not scorch. It moved away from the knot, drifting to the right side of my chest, and soon became a whisper of heat that could be felt but not felt as an urgent need. It was easier to watch this shift, to note that the desire was not a fixed thing but a changing presence that could be seen, not begged for.

The practice was just seeing. I watched the knot loosen, watched the craving drift, watched both still there yet altered. I was not chasing an end; I was observing the law of interdependence in the flesh. The moment the knot relaxed, the breath moved deeper without effort. The craving’s warmth faded with each exhale. The stillness that emerged was not a state to be achieved, but a simple awareness of the present.
