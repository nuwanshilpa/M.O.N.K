A coolness brushes the nostrils on the in-breath, neutral and brief. On the out-breath a warmth lingers at the upper lip, also neutral. Somewhere in the chest a faint ache forms — dull, spreading, neither sharp nor urgent. The mind tags it unpleasant, then watches the tag dissolve as the ache shifts to a faint pressure, then to nothing. A bird calls outside; the sound arrives pleasant, a bright thread, and before the label settles the thread snaps into silence. Another breath. The palms rest on the knees, skin against cotton, a neutral contact that flickers into a tiny itch at the wrist — unpleasant for a heartbeat — then gone. No reaching toward the pleasant, no pushing from the unpleasant, no ignoring the neutral. Each tone rises, is known, passes. The knowing itself does not stay.
