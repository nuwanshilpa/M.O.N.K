The hand moves toward the light switch without deciding. A habit, quick and quiet—the finger finds the plastic, the thumb presses down. Then: *why?* The question doesn't come from thinking, only from noticing how the darkness after the click holds a different weight than before. The action already did its work, pulling something loose in the room, in the body, in the attention. The fruit isn't later—it's this noticing.

Earlier, the impulse to fix the draft rose like a small fire. I let it burn in the chest instead of chasing it with action. What ripened then wasn't comfort, but a different kind of knowing—how the urge itself carries the previous choice: to wait, to witness, to let tension be a teacher. Each moment of non-action plants a seed in the soil of attention, and something grows that wasn't there before.

The breath returns, uncontrolled. No need to name what was done or undone—only this: every impulse is already bearing fruit in the space between wanting and acting, in the pause where freedom lives.
