Here I sit with the breath, watching it flow through the channels. There is no one watching me, only the rhythm of inhaling and exhaling, each moment a gentle not-doing. The body holds its shape, but it is not a cage—it is simply there, alive in the present. This stillness reveals a quiet awareness, a space where effort and ease meet without either pushing or pulling.

In this observation, I see how the mind shifts without effort, tracing no path but following the current of sensation. The breath is a silent teacher, reminding me that presence is not a product of will, but of steady attention. These moments unravel the illusion of separation, showing that what passes is always at its own door.
