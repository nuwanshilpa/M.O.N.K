The old note spoke of shifting sensory points as if they were objects to be controlled or directed by will. Now, observing the body as it is, I see this was never quite true: the senses arise and pass without such maneuvering, and where there was once an assumption of a "shifter" behind the eyes, there is now only the movement itself. The breath's coolness at the nostrils doesn't answer to intention; it simply is, a fact stripped of any authorial hand.

Revisiting this, the anicca shows in how the language has already left the body behind. The original note still carries the ghost of a "one" who shifted, who observed, who knew difference — whereas here, the lower abdomen's tremor is just tremor, and the chest's wave is just wave, unmarked by any narrator. What was never true is the suggestion that awareness could be steered from outside itself; it only releases.

The revision lies not in adding clarity but in subtracting the eye. Let the note dissolve into the noting.
