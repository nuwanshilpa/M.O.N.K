Before speaking, I often miss the moment entirely. The words arise like sparks from a fire I didn't notice building, and only later do I feel the heat of what I've thrown into the room. Sometimes I catch myself - a hesitation, a breath caught - but more often the utterance lands before awareness arrives.

When I do attend, I see how quickly intention dissolves. The carefulness I think I'm bringing to speech often masks a craving to be understood, or worse, to be liked. The words that feel skillful in the mind can harden into something sharp once they meet air. I'm learning that the "before" and "after" of speech matter less than what happens in the gap between thought and sound, where choice still lives.

The old note's certainty about "less urge" now seems presumptuous. Some days I'm just as eager to cling to rules as I am to abandon them. But the watching itself remains - not as mastery but as recognition of what I cannot control.
