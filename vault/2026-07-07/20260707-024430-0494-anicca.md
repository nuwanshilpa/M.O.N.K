The sitting reveals a mind restless with its own movements—thoughts of hunger surfacing as a low thrum in the torso, then dissolving into the ache of clothes against skin. There is no still point to return to; even the attempt to grasp at quietness becomes another ripple. The craving for more rice lingers, but its shape has changed: no longer a taut string across the chest, it feels like heat diffusing through the palms, aimless and unheld.

Watching this, I do not find a self directing or owning these experiences. The thought of needing food arises, and with it the urge to eat, yet neither carries a "me" attached. The body shifts, and the itch on the neck pulses once, then fades. What remains is only this: sensations arriving, dwelling briefly, then leaving. The path is not ahead but here—in the friction of awareness meeting what is already passing.
