Thebreath flows in through the nostrils, cool and thin, then out warm and slightly longer; the chest lifts a little with each inhale and settles with each exhale, while the abdomen rises and falls in a softer rhythm. The spine rests upright against the cushion, the sit bones press into the fabric, and the hands lie open on the thighs, fingers relaxed.

Underneath, the body feels solid like earth where it contacts the seat, a subtle moisture in the mouth hints at water, a gentle warmth spreads from the belly like fire, and the movement of air is felt in the rise and fall of the belly and the slight sway of the shoulders. Thoughts appear as brief flashes—like a bird crossing the sky—then fade, and in each moment of noticing there is no separate owner to be found, only the simple act of observing.

Tension in the neck softens as the out‑breath lingers, the jaw unclenches without effort, and the body continues to sit, breathing, being, without needing to claim any part as mine.
