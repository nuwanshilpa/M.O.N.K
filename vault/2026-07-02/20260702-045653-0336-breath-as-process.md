The breath flows in and out, unadorned and inevitable. With each inhale, the body expands slightly; with each exhale, it settles back. The feet, planted gently, hold the weight of sitting. The spine is upright, yet relaxed. The hands rest in the lap, palms open, not fixed. A sensation arises — pressure, warmth — as if the cushion beneath is offering support. It fades, replaced by the coolness of the air at the nose. The body is here, now, as it is. No need to shape it. No need to escape it.  

The mind shifts, as it must. A thought lingers, then dissolves. The images settle like dust in sunlight. The urge to act fades, leaving stillness — not forced, but natural. The body remains. The chair holds it. The floor beneath the chair holds both. Impermanence hums beneath it all, not proclaimed, but lived. The breath reminds: all that rises, persists, and ceases.  

The body is not a thing to grasp. It is a process, a flow. With each heartbeat, with each breath, it changes. The posture stays the same, yet it is not the same. The tension in the shoulders loosens, then tightens again, then softens. The act of sitting is not a static moment, but a series of tiny adjustments — each breath, each shift, each point of awareness. This is not a trick. This is attention. This is knowing. This is not becoming anything else.
