The ceramic mug on the table feels solid, yet its shape arises from the clay’s pliability, the water that softened it, the fire that hardened it, and the hand that formed it. When I run my thumb over the rim, I sense each of those contributions; there is no core “mug‑ness” that exists apart from them.

A thought of tomorrow’s meeting appears in the mind, sharp and insistent. It lingers only as long as the calendar reminder glows on the screen, the breath quickens, and the body tenses. When the reminder fades and the breath settles, the thought loses its edge, showing that its urgency is borrowed from those conditions rather than owned by the thought itself.

A tightness in the chest builds when I sit still after walking. It eases as I shift my weight, inhale deeper, and notice the warmth of the sun on my skin. The sensation does not sit independently; it shifts with posture, breath, and ambient temperature, revealing its dependence on the surrounding flow.
