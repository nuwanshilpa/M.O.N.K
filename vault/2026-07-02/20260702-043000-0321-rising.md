The breath enters the nostrils, cool and thin, then leaves with a slight warmth. As the lungs expand, the chest rises, pushing against the fabric of this worn robe. There is a heavy sensation in the thighs where they meet the hard ground, a weight that feels solid, yet it pulses with a constant, microscopic tremor.

The inhalation is followed by a brief, empty pause before the exhale. In that pause, there is a tension, a subtle holding of the ribs, an attempt to maintain the stillness. But the breath cannot be held; it inevitably collapses and must give way to the next pull of air. The body tries to settle into a comfortable posture, but the spine eventually drifts, and a small ache blooms at the base of the neck. This ache arises, shifts its shape, and eventually fades into a dullness, never staying as one thing for more than a moment.
