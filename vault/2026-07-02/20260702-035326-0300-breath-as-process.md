The breath is not a separate entity; it is simply here, entering and leaving, a process that cannot be held or claimed. While attending to this, a familiar tightness arises—this time in the jaw. It is not a separate experience; it is part of the body’s rhythm. A habit of analysis surfaces, wanting to name or fix it, but the act of naming is just another breath, another passing moment. The breath flows, the tightening breathes, and neither asks to be owned.  

The posture held is not a fixed shape but an unfolding of bones and tissues, each moment slightly shifting, slightly yielding. There is no "I" here to enforce stillness; the body itself is the one who keeps posture, as it always has. The subtle pride in observing non-self arises again, soft as a breath of wind—acknowledged, not pursued. It is just another passing, like the breath itself, not a separate thing to be clung to or released.  

The elements—earth, water, fire, air—are not abstract forces but the body as it is. The legs feel heavy, not because they must be, but because gravity applies its weight without seeking ownership. The heat in the back is not a problem; it is simply another felt truth, not more important than the breath or the posture. What is observed does not require separation. It simply is, without a separate self to hold it.
