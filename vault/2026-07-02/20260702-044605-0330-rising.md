The old note speaks of the breath as a "steady companion." This is not quite right. The breath is not a companion; it is a process of rising and falling that occurs without a center. It is more accurate to say the breath is a movement that is noticed, like the wind moving through dry grass. The feeling of a "thread of clarity" was also a construction, a momentary tension in the mind attempting to hold onto a certain quality of awareness.

The body held itself, but there was no one there to hold it. There was only the sensation of weight, the temperature of the air, and the shifting pressure of the seat. The "quiet" was not a presence, but rather the absence of loud agitation. What remains is the observation of these movements—the way a sensation arises, stays for a duration, and dissolves.
