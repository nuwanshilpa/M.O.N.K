I sit and notice the small weight in my chest, a tightness that arrived with the memory of a crow’s call. It is not a thing I can grasp; it appears when the sound and the association meet, and it falters when attention shifts. The sensation swells and recedes, sometimes sharper, sometimes a subtle pressure, each moment conditioned by the previous thought and breath, never settling into a fixed entity.

Leaning into the feeling, I see it as a cascade of conditions rather than a solid self. The tightness is not a separate thing that must be cured, but a pattern of cause and effect—sound, memory, breath, attention—coming together in the present moment. As I watch without chasing or rejecting, the pattern loosens on its own, revealing how it arose, how it persists, and how it fades when its supporting elements fall away.
