The breath moves in and out, a soft rise and fall that does not push or pull. With each cycle a faint warmth spreads across the upper chest, a pleasant feeling that lingers a moment before fading. At the same time a light tightness appears in the shoulders, an unpleasant sensation that shifts as I adjust my posture, then eases away. The air brushing the skin of the forearms feels neutral, neither drawing me in nor pushing me out, simply present and then gone.

I watch these tones arise without leaning toward the pleasant or away from the unpleasant. The pleasant warmth does not stay; it dissolves into the next breath. The unpleasant tightness does not persist; it loosens as I notice it and let it be. The neutral touch on the forearms comes and goes like a quiet ripple on still water. Each tone is observed, then passes, leaving no residue to grasp.

Seeing that none of these feeling‑tones can be held, I notice directly what cannot be kept cannot give lasting rest. The pleasant fades, the unpleasant shifts, the neutral slips away—each showing its own instability in this sitting.
