The hunger is a knot in the stomach, a heat that demands to be cooled. I try to hold onto the sensation of the breath, the way it feels smooth in the throat, but the smoothness is already thinning. As soon as the inhale reaches its peak, it begins to fail, turning into an exhale. There is no point in the breath where it stays; it is a constant shedding of what was just happening.

When I try to grasp the warmth in the chest, it is like trying to catch a shadow. The warmth arrives, settles for a heartbeat, and then the coolness of the air rushes in to replace it. There is a repetitive grasping—a small, internal tightening—whenever a pleasant sensation starts to move toward its end. This tightening is the friction of trying to make a passing moment stay.
