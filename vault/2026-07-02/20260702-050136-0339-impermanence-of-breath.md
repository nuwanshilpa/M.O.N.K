The breath moves like water through a narrow channel: cool at the entrance, warming as it fills the chest, then spilling into the belly’s rise and fall. Each inhale swells the cavity, each exhale empties it, a rhythm that never repeats exactly. Watching this flow, you notice how the body holds the breath only until the next exhale, how the sensation fades the moment it is named.

When the mind reaches for the breath as something to keep, a subtle tension appears, a clinging that feels like pressure. Letting go of that grip reveals a quiet space where the breath simply passes, without need for ownership. In that space the law of impermanence shows itself, and the ending of craving becomes visible as the breath dissolves into the next moment.

The practice is not a theory but a repeated noticing: each cycle of inhalation and exhalation is a chance to see the truth of suffering, its cause, its cessation, and the way that leads to that cessation. It is the same path that has been walked before, now met with fresh attention.
