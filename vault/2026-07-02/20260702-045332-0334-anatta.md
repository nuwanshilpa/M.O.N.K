The back of the thighs presses into the cushion, a dull ache that comes and goes with each small shift. A bird calls outside, and the sound carries a slight pleasant tinge that fades within seconds. The mind reaches for another sound, another sensation to hold, but each arrival already slips away.

The wanting itself is felt as a tightening in the chest, not as a thought about wanting. When this is noticed, the grip loosens slightly. There is no one who relaxes, only the relaxing. The impermanence is not an idea but the actual texture of what is felt here: nothing stays, nothing can be gathered and kept.
