Breath enters cool at the nostrils, warmer leaving. The belly rises, falls — no one breathing, just the rising and falling. Shoulders hold a familiar knot, right side tighter. When attention rests there, the knot doesn't soften; it simply becomes known as pressure, heat, a small throb that shifts when the breath passes through. The spine straightens on its own, then slumps, then straightens. No commander issuing orders.

An itch blooms on the left ankle. The urge to scratch arises — a tiny surge of energy in the fingers, a leaning forward in the mind. Watching without moving, the itch peaks, fractures into pins and needles, then dissolves. The urge was not mine; it arose, played out, vanished. Same with the thought about dinner: a flash of rice, steam, hunger — then gone, leaving only the breath's rhythm. Each sensation, each impulse, appears complete unto itself, ownerless.

The body sits, a collection of pressures, temperatures, movements. Earth in the bones, water in the saliva, fire in the belly's warmth, wind in the breath. Space holds it all. No "body" found anywhere — only these changing qualities, knowing themselves.
