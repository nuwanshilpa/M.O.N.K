The mud that clumps under my feet lifts when I pause to feel it, not to name it. I watch the mud settle, then watch myself step over it, feeling the weight shift from one foot to the other. The mud is the action, the settling the fruit. In the pause, I see that each step that turns the mud into soil is both a cause and a result. The soil remains until the next footfall, a small ripple that grows into a path.

In the quiet, the stillness of my breath does not vanish but expands, filling the gaps between actions. I notice the breath that falls after the step, the one that climbs when I inhale again. The breath is not separate from the mud; it is the river that rescues the mud from becoming stone. The river carries the mud, the mud provides the river with nourishment, and the river continues to flow.

The observation does not label the mud as good or bad; it simply notes the sequence: push, leave, pull, repeat. Each push is a seed of future soil, each pull a promise that soil will be present when next needed. The learning is that my stepping is both causing and receiving, and the soil is always a few breaths away.
