The hand reaches for the cup without asking. There's a coolness in the throat, a wetness sliding down, and already the mind notes: pleasant. This is just the body taking what it needs. But then there's something else — a subtle tightening, a reaching forward in the chest space that wasn't there before, as if the pleasant feeling has opened a door and something has slipped through it.

The sun moves across the floor. Shadows shift. The body settles into the chair, then shifts again, seeking the right angle. Each adjustment brings relief, but relief is not the same as satisfaction. There's always another pressure to address, another itch to scratch. The wanting arises with the feeling, not after it — they seem to emerge together, like breath and its awareness.

When the wanting is noticed, it changes. It doesn't disappear, but it softens at the edges. The reaching becomes more careful, more deliberate. The pull toward pleasant feeling and away from discomfort loosens just enough to see itself as a movement, not a command. This seeing is not mine, but neither is the wanting that continues its quiet work.
