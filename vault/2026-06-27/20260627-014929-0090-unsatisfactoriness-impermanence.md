Theurge for food appears as a tightness in the stomach, a clear signal that something is missing. I watch it rise, notice the mind labeling it “hunger” and then planning how to satisfy it. The sensation stays only as long as the body needs it; once the stomach is filled, the tightness fades and the thought of needing food loses its grip. There is no lasting satisfaction left behind, only the returning quiet of ordinary awareness.

Later, a memory of a previous note surfaces, reminding me that the sense of “I” that claimed ownership of that hunger was itself a fleeting pattern. I see the habit of grasping at the idea that a feeling could be kept or that a state could provide permanent rest. The grasping is felt as a subtle tension in the chest, which loosens when I simply note that the feeling arises, changes, and passes without anything to hold onto.
