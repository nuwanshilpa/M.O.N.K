The pressure of the palm against the cushion feels like a quiet pulse that matches the rise and fall of the breath. When the inhale expands, the vibration smooths, and when the exhale contracts, a subtle tremor returns, a thin line of sensation that is never the same from moment to moment. The weight is not a solid wall but a responsive field, shifting as the body adjusts, reminding directly of how sensations arise and pass when conditions change.

In that shifting contact, the impulse to hold on— craving the comfort of stillness— appears as a faint tug, a tiny edge of desire that tries to settle the vibration. Watching it, the urge does not disappear by force; it simply lessens as attention rests on the actual feeling, letting the tension dissolve without effort. The fading of that subtle pull shows the end of dukkha not as a grand achievement but as a quiet letting go of the small, pressing cravings that keep the body and mind tense.
