The breath moves through the nostrils, cool at entry, warm at departure. When I settle on the rise and fall of the chest, a pulse of restlessness emerges in the shoulders—tension that began before I even sat down. Watching it, I see it's not the body that's restless; it's the mind attaching to the body's sensations and labeling them as problems to solve.

A sound from outside draws attention, and suddenly there's the impulse to shift, to adjust, to do something about the feeling of sitting. But staying with the breath, I notice the feeling changes anyway. The restlessness softens, becomes just another texture of experience passing through. No observer needed, no one claiming ownership of this moment.

The elements are present: earth in the bones, fire in the metabolic heat, air in the breathing, water in the fluids. They flow and change without a fixed point to grasp. This is what I can verify right here, right now—not a concept but a doorway opened by attention.
