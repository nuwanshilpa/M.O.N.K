Themind feels like a fist that won't unclench. Each breath comes in, and the fingers tighten around it — mine, mine, mine — before the exhale pries them loose again. Not a dramatic struggle, just the quiet insistence of ownership staking its claim on what was never held. A sound outside: a truck downshifting on the highway. The mind jumps to name it, then to the neighbor's dog barking last week, then to the vet bill, then to the thought that I should call my brother. All in the space of two breaths. The scattering isn't chaotic; it's methodical, almost tender in its persistence.

Underneath the chatter, a dull ache sits in the chest — not sadness exactly, more like the bruise left by leaning too long against a doorframe. Craving wears a thousand masks: the want for quiet, the want for insight, the want for this sitting to mean something. Each mask dissolves when looked at directly, only to reform a moment later in a slightly different shape. The mind knows it's doing this. It watches itself manufacture dissatisfaction out of pure motion. No one is driving. The truck passes. The dog stops. The ache remains, pulsing with each heartbeat, neither enemy nor friend.
