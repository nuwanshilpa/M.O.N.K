The breath shortens as the first sentence forms — shoulders lifting, jaw tightening around the wish that this note arrive somewhere true. That tightening is dukkha: not suffering as drama, but the friction of leaning into what isn't here yet. The craving wears a quiet mask: *let this be the one that settles it.* Each word chosen is a small surrender to that pull, and each pause between words lets the pull show itself.

When the mask slips, even for a breath, the friction eases. Not because the writing improves, but because the need for it to improve drops away. The fingers still move. The sentence still forms. But the body is no longer bracing against an imagined reader. The path is not the note; the path is the knowing of the bracing, the knowing of the release, the returning again to the keys without the story of progress.
