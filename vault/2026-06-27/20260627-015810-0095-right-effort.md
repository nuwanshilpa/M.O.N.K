The path is not a distant road, but the texture of the friction currently occurring. When I sit, there is the tension in the thighs, then the itch on the shoulder, then the urge to shift. Each movement is a step. I see the craving for comfort arise as a tightening, and the effort to remain still as a counter-tightening. The path is found in the middle of that tugging, where the tension is seen for what it is—a sequence of rising and falling impulses.

There is no singular "right way" to walk, only the observation of how the feet meet the ground and how the mind attempts to leap ahead to the next step. When the mind wanders toward a memory, the path is the moment the wandering is noticed. The correction is not a forceful pull, but a simple redirection of attention. The sequence of intention, action, and result is visible in the way a thought leads to a tightening of the jaw, which leads to a dull ache.
