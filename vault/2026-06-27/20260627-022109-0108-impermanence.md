The mind wants to settle into something solid, something I can lean on. It reaches back for that pond-image, hoping it will hold steady, but the water keeps moving. A memory of yesterday's alms-giving comes -- the sour bread, the woman's tired smile -- and I watch wanting arise to make it mean something, to make it matter more than it does. The wanting itself feels tight, like a fist forming.

There is no holding the sitting. No keeping the breath or the attention or the intention. The thigh tension shifts, the mind flits to planning tomorrow's walk, then returns with relief. Even the relief cannot stay. Each state carries its own fading built in, like a shadow lengthening. When I try to grasp the stillness, it becomes another object of craving, another thing that cannot satisfy because it was never mine to keep.
