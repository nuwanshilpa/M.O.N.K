As I sit, the breath enters and leaves, a gentle swell and release in the chest. The posture, held with effort, brings a tension to the thighs and a subtle ache in the lower back. The elements at play - the warmth of the air, the pressure of the seat on the skin - are constantly shifting, never quite settling into a stable state.

In this sitting, the mark of unsatisfactoriness reveals itself in the restless desire to adjust, to shift and relieve the discomfort. The itch on the shoulder, the tickle in the nose, each sensation gives rise to a craving for change, for relief. The urge to move, to scratch or stretch, is a palpable force, a constant tug on the attention. And yet, as I attend to the body, I see that each movement, each adjustment, brings only temporary respite, and the discomfort returns, a reminder that what cannot be kept cannot give lasting rest.

The breath, the posture, the sensations, all are in constant flux, and in this flux, I see the impermanence of all things, the impossibility of finding lasting comfort in a body that is subject to the whims of sensation and the forces of nature. links: Impermanence, Restlessness
