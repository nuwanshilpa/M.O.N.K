The mat held the weight of the body for a moment, a concrete pressure that changed as the hips shifted and the spine curved. That pressure was not a constant; it rose, softened, and moved with each adjustment, showing how a single sensation can dissolve when the condition changes.

Near the throat a subtle tightening began, a ripple of muscle tone that built without naming itself. It felt like a held‑in feeling, yet it never declared itself as a thought; it was simply a shift in the body’s internal map, appearing and fading with the breath.

When I let that tension be the object of attention, the idea of a separate, lasting “self” in the pressure fell away. The tightening was seen as a temporary alignment of forces, dependent on breath, posture, and awareness
