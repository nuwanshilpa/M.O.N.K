I sat with the spine upright, feet planted, a hand on the knees. The breath entered through the nose, the chest lifted, the abdomen deepened, and then the exhale withdrew. The body remained a single configuration, the weight balanced on the seat, shoulders settled, collarbone a few centimetres from the back. I noted how the breath was a ripple, not a wave; each inhalation was a slight expansion, each exhalation a gentle contraction. The breath did not knock on the back of the mind; it simply rose and fell, as a river does, with no resistance.

With each exhale I felt a small loosening of the muscles that had been holding tight. The shoulders sank a hair lower, the knees relaxed a little more. In the quiet between breaths I saw the same body, the same posture, the same sensations, without any sense of a separate “I” watching. The sensory map did not attach a label of owner to the experience; it was just a sequence of changes, a stream of states that came and went.

When the breath finished the cycle I was left with the stillness of the seat and the lightness of the skin. No thought of “I am here” arose; instead the body simply existed as the sum of its parts in the moment. The ordinary pattern of pain, tension, and relief was present, but I was not the source of it. The body sat there, a temporary assembly, and the sense of self folded back into the ordinary flow of sensations.
