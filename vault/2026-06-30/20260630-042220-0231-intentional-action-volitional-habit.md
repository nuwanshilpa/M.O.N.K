The hand reaches for the doorknob, fingers curling around cool metal. In that instant, a thought arises: I should be gentler today. The action completes itself, but the seed of intention remains—some days it sprouts as careless slamming, other days as careful quiet. I watched this afternoon as impatience with the slow wifi led to clicking the trackpad too hard, which led to a sharp email sent too quickly, which led to sitting here now feeling the familiar weight of regret.

This is what the teaching points toward: each volitional act plants something in the ground of this body-mind. The fruit ripens not in some distant future but in the very next moment's quality of presence. When speech flows from contracted shoulders and clenched jaw, the room tightens around it. When walking stems from relaxed hips and open chest, the hallway softens. I am always standing on what I have sown, even when I pretend otherwise.
