The breathmoves in and out. No one commands it. The body rests on the mat, not perfectly still — a shoulder rolls, the jaw unclenches, the belly rises and falls. In the throat, a faint constriction. It isn't separate from the impulse to reach for the phone; they rise together, a single movement of contraction. The old note said the tightening comes "always just before the thought." That was a story laid over the raw experience afterward. In the moment, there is no before — only the tightness and the reaching, known together.

When the constriction eases, the breath does not necessarily grow softer. Sometimes it does. Sometimes the next breath catches again. The urge to check the phone does not dissolve "as soon" — the note broke off there, unfinished. The urge fades, returns, fades. No clean resolution. The earlier writing smoothed the ragged edge into a teaching moment. That smoothing was the very craving it claimed to observe.
