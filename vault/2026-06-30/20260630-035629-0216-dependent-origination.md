The breath comes in and a warmth spreads across the chest. I notice that warmth as a single sensation, a discrete feeling that is there when I look at it. Immediate after that, the throat tightens, a pull that feels as if it is trying to keep the warmth from moving on. The tightening itself is a new sensation that I see arise out of the earlier warmth.

When the warmth fades—when the breath goes out and the feeling of heat diminishes—the throat no longer tightens. The tightening stops in the same instant as the warmth stops. The two arise together and cease together, as if one is always conditioned by the other. I observe this as a simple chain: contact produces feeling, which produces a conditioned reaction; when contact ends, the chain ends.
