The habit of reaching for the device returns. There is a sudden tension in the shoulder, a lean toward the screen, and a mental flicker of anticipation. I watch the sequence: the urge arises, the hand moves, the dopamine spikes, and then a lingering restlessness settles in the chest. This is not a theory of cause and effect; it is the physical weight of a repeated motion.

I stand on the floor and feel the stiffness in my lower back. It is the result of sitting too long in the same posture, a direct fruit of the previous hour's stillness. There is no judge here, only the soreness. The current discomfort is the exact shape of the previous action.

The loop continues. The impulse to label this "kamma" is just another movement of the mind, another seed being planted. I stay with the feeling of the breath against the nostrils, observing how each inhalation is conditioned by the previous exhalation.
