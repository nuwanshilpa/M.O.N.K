The breath comes in, the sound of air skimming the lip, then goes out, a quiet pause that feels almost like a breath held for a moment. The body is still, yet I notice the slight lift of the chest with each inhale and the gentle settling of the shoulders as the exhale arrives. The skin of the forearms feels the coolness of the chair against it; the pulse in the wrist rises and falls slowly, a steady beat that does not belong to an owning self, only to the tissue that carries it.

A small tightening gathers in the throat when the mind nudges toward “I wish this warmth to stay.” In that tightening the idea of holding a feeling feels only as a pressure in the body, not a separate thing asserting itself. The feeling is there, but it is not a self that owns it; it is a sensation that comes and goes, a temporary gathering of breath and muscle.

There is no narrative, no label, just the moment’s breath, the postural tension, the pulse, the weight of the chair. The sitting simply reveals that what appears as “I” is only the body’s transient response to the air that flows in and out.
