The teaching sits here like a stone in the palm: actions leave traces, and those traces become the ground underfoot. I notice how the morning’s small choice—to sit rather than reach for the phone—settles into a faint steadiness in the chest, a quiet weight that wasn’t there yesterday. It isn’t a reward; it’s simply what remains, like the smell of smoke after a fire has passed. The ground shifts under each step, and I am walking on what I’ve already done, not what I intend.

When a sharp wish for comfort arises, I see it land on that ground and change it slightly, the way a footfall presses a mark into damp earth. The mark will dry, or the next rain will smooth it, but for now it holds the shape of that moment’s leaning. I don’t need to call it mine or not-mine; I only see that the leaning left a groove, and the next step will find that groove already there, guiding the foot before the mind decides.
