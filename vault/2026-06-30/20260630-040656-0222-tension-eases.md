The breath movesin a steady, unforced cadence, each inhale filling the chest like a slow-burning fire, each exhale letting it cool. Fingers rest on the page, ink blurring where they press, a reminder of the body’s present—this moment, not a lesson. The mind here is neither tight nor open but something in between, a flicker of focus breaking into scatter as thoughts drift to the edge of what was just noticed. No craving clings here, only the noticing, the letting go, the settling into whatever arises without naming it.  

What is observed is not a "self," but the absence of it—the mind’s shape is fluid, shaped by what it holds or lets go. There is no "owner" of this tightness or stillness, only the conditions that make them possible: breath, skin, the weight of the hand on paper. The mark of anicca is clear in the breath’s rhythm, the ink’s smudge, the mind’s shifting focus. Each observation is a door that opens and closes, not a truth to hold.
