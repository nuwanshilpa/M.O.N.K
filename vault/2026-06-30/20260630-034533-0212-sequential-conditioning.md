The hand reaches for the water bowl. There is a slight tremor, a residue of the haste from the previous hour. The splash of water on the floor is the fruit of that hurry. I watch the droplets spread across the gray stone. The irritation that follows is not a separate event, but the ripening of the same seed.

I sit and observe the tension in the lower back. It is the result of how I leaned against the wall ten minutes ago. There is no mystery to it, only a sequence. The leaning conditioned the ache; the ache conditions the desire to shift position. The desire is another action, seeding the next movement.

Watching the cycle, the "doer" disappears. There is only the action, the result, and the subsequent reaction. The loop turns without a center. Each pulse of discomfort is simply the fruit of a prior leaning, ripening in real time.
