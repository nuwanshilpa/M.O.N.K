The impulse to speak often arrives as a tightening in the throat, a sudden heat that demands to be released. When I observe this, I see the speech being shaped by a desire to be heard or to correct. There is a pull toward words that are clever or words that wound. I watch the sensation of the "right" word forming in the mind, noting how it feels like a grasping, a reaching to bridge a gap between myself and another.

When I wait, the words lose their jagged edges. The urge to speak impulsively cools into a duller, more manageable sensation. I see the way a sharp word would have functioned like a sudden contraction in the chest, while a timely, quiet word allows the breath to remain even. The difference is not a moral choice made in the head, but a visible shift in the tension of the body.
