The breath moves in its own rhythm, a wave that neither insists nor resists. In the space between inhalations, there is a stillness where tension softens, not erased but allowed to ease into itself. Each cycle of breath carries the memory of earlier breaths, not as a burden but as a quiet shaping—like water remembering its path. The body’s resilience here is not a triumph but a condition, evident in how small adjustments—like adjusting posture or simply letting the hands rest—can quiet the mind enough to notice the breath’s own quiet endurance.  

The mind, too, flows with a subtle agitation. Thoughts arise and dissolve, some leaving a faint residue of discomfort, others dissolving without trace. A knee ache, for instance, appears and fades, untethered to any particular ownership or history. This impermanence is not phrased as a truth but observed directly in the sensation itself—the way the ache has no anchor, no definable endpoint. Dissatisfaction arises not as a conclusion but as a quality of what is given, marked by its inability to hold.
