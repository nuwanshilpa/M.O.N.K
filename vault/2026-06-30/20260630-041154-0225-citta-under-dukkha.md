The breath moves in and out like a rope gently pulled taut, then released. I try to locate the feeler of this breath, the watcher behind the eyes who thinks it's controlling this passage. But the more closely I look, the more the looking itself becomes just another sensation—eyelids fluttering, nostrils flaring slightly, air meeting skin. No owner appears.

There's a pressure behind the sternum, not quite discomfort, more like the body remembering it once held something called "I." I name it—memory, longing, the echo of a voice that used to say "mine" whenever it saw the breath, claimed it as proof of presence. The naming doesn't make it disappear; it makes it smaller, lighter, until even the pressure is just pressure, not pressure *of* anything.

What remains is the attending, steady as the space between heartbeats. Not the absence of something, but the presence of this—unclaimed, unwelcome to no one, belonging to nothing. The breath continues, and the one who thought it belonged to him vanishes into the next breath, leaving only the rhythm, the pause, the next moment's unnamed door opening.
