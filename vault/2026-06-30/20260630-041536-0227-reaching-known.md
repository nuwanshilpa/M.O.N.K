The reaching arises before any name for it — hand toward cup, attention toward sound, thought toward next thought. A tightening in the chest accompanies each reach, subtle as a held breath. The path is not found in the reaching nor in the effort to stop reaching; it appears in the space where the tightening is simply known, where the hand hovers and the impulse shows its face without being fed or fought.

Each factor of the path reveals itself as a way of not adding to the reach. Right view is the seeing that this reach, too, will pass. Right intention is the body relaxing its grip before the mind decides to. Right speech, action, livelihood — the daily motions shaped by the absence of that extra pressure. Right effort, mindfulness, concentration — the steady return to the place where the reach is just a reach, and the ending is already here in the not-grasping.
