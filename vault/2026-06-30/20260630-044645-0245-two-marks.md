Right now the body holds its shape, a quiet question without answer. The breath moves softly, yet each inhalation and exhalation feels like a small shift in the weight of being. There’s no sense of self in this—the feeling of a separate observer is soft, like mist on the edge of dawn. I notice the subtle stresses in my limbs, the way tension creeps in when the phone buzzes, and it passes without fixing anything. This is the ordinary ground, where attention rests without effort.

In this sitting, I see little things repeating: the rhythm of the breath, the shift of the weight, the faint ache of fatigue. There’s no need to change or control; just watch what comes and goes. The mind is a gentle ripple, not a wave. These observations return again and again, like ripples in still water.
