The air is thinner now, and the heat in the shoulders has shifted to a dull ache in the lower back. Looking back at the old note, the claim that "no observer" was needed was a conceptual gloss. There was still a subtle pride in the act of watching, a hidden "I" pretending to be the one who noticed the absence of an "I." The observation was a description of a state, not the state itself.

The breath remains: cool, warm, rising, falling. The tension in the back is just a pressure, a density that fluctuates. When the impulse to shift arises, it is simply a movement of intent, not a problem to be solved or a texture to be softened. There is only the pressure, the intent to move, and the sound of a distant fan.

The elements are not a doorway or a model; they are just the heat and the hardness of the seat. The bones are heavy. The breath is shallow. The note is just ink and light, changing as the eye moves across it.
