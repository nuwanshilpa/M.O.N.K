A thin tightness rises in the chest as the wind continues to scrape the windowpane. It is a feeling that wants to act, a quick pull toward fixing the draft, yet I stay with the sensation itself. I note the tone—sharp, brief, a little uncomfortable—without reaching for a solution, letting it come and go like a wave that arrives and lifts again.

From this watching, the unsatisfactoriness becomes visible: the tug appears, lingers just long enough to be felt, then dissolves without leaving a lasting peace. It cannot be held, cannot be satisfied, and its passing shows the inherent lack of permanence in the experience. The impulse is not a command, but a fleeting condition that simply arises and falls.
