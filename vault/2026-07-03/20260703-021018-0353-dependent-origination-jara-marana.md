Breath moves in cool at the nostrils, warm as it leaves; the belly lifts and settles without a doer. A familiar tightness sits in the right shoulder, a steady pressure that does not soften when attention rests on it, but is simply felt as pressure, heat, a subtle resistance in the muscles.

A sharp sting flares in the left knee, an unpleasant heat that presses against the stillness. For a few breaths the mind reaches out, trying to push the feeling away, but the sensation already begins to change, losing its edge and spreading into a dull warmth. When the urge to grasp fades, the heat no longer pushes against the quiet; it is felt as a passing tone, neither clung to nor resisted.

The breath continues, the shoulder pressure remains known, the knee sensation arises and fades. Each appearance depends on a prior condition — attention, intention, the body's own state — and each disappearance follows when that condition ceases
