The breath moved in and out of the nostrils, cool on the inhale, warm on the exhale, while the chest rose and fell with each cycle. The back was straight, shoulders relaxed, the weight of the body settled into the floorboards. The four elements were present — earth as the solid seat, water as the flowing blood, fire as the heat of the skin, air as the spaciousness between the ribs — each changing without effort.

When a thought of the next task entered, a tightening rose in the chest, a subtle agitation that made the breath feel shallow. The mind darted to the upcoming workload, then slipped back to the breath, never staying long enough to settle. In that moment the body showed clearly what cannot be kept, and the dissatisfaction arose directly from the tightening itself, not from any label.
