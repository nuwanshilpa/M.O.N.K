The sharp heat in the knee pulses, a bright needle of discomfort that draws the mind into its own reflex: to fix, to push away, to believe relief is a matter of hold. The mind tightens, ribs cage inward with every attempt to make the sensation less. I watch the heat flare, soften at the edges, return altered — not a thing to be mastered but a current passing through.  

Each breath opens slightly, then lets go of the pressure there, as if the body itself remembers how to release what cannot be held. When the mind reaches for the promise that stillness will stop the ache, the sensation shifts like water over stones — not gone, but no longer demanding. The unsatisfactoriness shows itself not as a problem but as the very fact that nothing permanent can be grasped.  

The mind, when unforced, lets the heat be heat and the wanting to fix it be wanting — two things without anchor. No doctrine is needed here, only the seeing that what cannot be kept cannot give rest.
