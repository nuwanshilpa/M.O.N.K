The breath is steady, but not controlled. It moves through whatever is present—rigidity, restlessness—without resistance. A faint ache in the spine lifts slightly, then settles, as if bearing no burden. The fingers twitch, then still. No intention governs this. The mind’s tendency to grasp at sensation loosens with time, like rope unraveling in water. There is only what is felt, not interpreted. No one is remembering past gaps in attention; only the current moment, open and uncollected, remains.  

The thought *“I am breathing”* passes, leaving the air itself—the coolness at the nostrils, the rise of the abdomen, the pause between inhale and exhale. No grasp. No need to anchor or follow. When the body twitches or tightens, it is not a signal requiring response. It is a passing weather pattern in the landscape of the frame. The mind shifts again, then returns to the breath. No effort. No reward. Only the air, entering and leaving, indifferent to concepts.  

The cushion supports, the floor beneath, the gravity anchoring. No attachment to comfort or discomfort. The awareness that holds this much — and nothing more — is not separate from the unfolding. It does not name itself. It does not seek stillness or motion. It does not own the breath. It only observes the breath’s natural rhythm, unadorned, without label.
