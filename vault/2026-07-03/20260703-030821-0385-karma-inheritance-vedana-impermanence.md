The foot remembers yesterday's path. Each step laid a stone in its memory — gravel, handle, breath. The ache now is not separate from that sequence; it is the sequence speaking. When I reach for understanding, when the mind presses close to the knee's dull throb, the feeling changes shape. Not because it is altered, but because grasping has weight.

Dukkha is this weight. Craving is the act of pressing closer, seeking resolution in the sensation itself. The Four Noble Truths are not a map here; they are the act of noticing how the seeking alters what is. The truth of ending is not somewhere else — it is the moment the hand withdraws from the knee, and the throb simply is, without meaning attached.
