The finger points at the moon — I watch it lift from the keyboard, muscles contracting, tendons tightening beneath skin. The movement carries intention: curiosity, the need to respond. Already the arm remembers this pattern, the shoulder rotates slightly differently than it did an hour ago, carrying forward the weight of previous movements. Each keystroke deposits a character, a small deletion another, and the screen holds what remains as impermanent as the breath that steadied before I began.

When anger arose earlier — that tightening in the chest, the impulse to lash out — I felt its fruits already ripening in the muscles, in the speed of my pulse. The action of feeling anger conditioned the next moment's breathing, the next thought's edge. Now, choosing to write instead of reacting, I plant seeds in the mental soil: patience, observation, release. These kammic formations do not bind; they condition what arises next.

The cursor blinks — a simple action repeating millions of times across the world — each click, each touch, each intentional deed ripening into consequence. Not karma as fate, but as the natural law of causation, as direct and immediate as the feeling in my knee, as fleeting as the breath that just left my body.
